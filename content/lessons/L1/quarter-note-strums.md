Good rhythm matters more than perfect chords: if the strums stay on the beat, a slightly fuzzy chord still sounds like music. This lesson locks your down-strums to the click.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: strum G with the strum player, counting "1, 2, 3, 4" aloud.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 46 BPM. Three clean loops in a row move you up 4 BPM.
- Review: 1 minute of G–D changes with the anchor finger.

"Clean" here means every strum on the beat, even if a note buzzes.

## One strum per beat

Count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud and strum down on each number. Your hand comes back up between strums without touching the strings.

::strum{pattern="D - D - D - D -" bpm=70 chord="G"}

## Practise with the click

The click always wins: if a chord isn't ready, use the open-string strum on beat 4 from the [E lesson](/learn/chord-e) and catch up.

::tool{name="metronome" bpm=70}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Stopping the strumming hand while the fretting hand changes. The strumming hand never stops.
- Speeding up on easy chords and slowing down on hard ones.
- Strumming from the wrist alone and tiring quickly: let the forearm swing from the elbow.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the exercise at 70 BPM.
