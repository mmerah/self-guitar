A solo follows the chords: the same box sounds different over A7, D7 and E7. Goal: a full written 12-bar solo in box 1 at 80 BPM, knowing where you are in the form.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the four-bar opening phrase alone.
- 5 minutes: the whole solo from 52 BPM up to 80, counting bars.
- 2 minutes: play the solo again, changing one phrase to your own.
- Review: one pass of the bend exercise.

## Target notes

Every chord has a home note in box 1. End your phrases on them and the solo sounds as if it knows the song:

- **Over A7:** A (string 4 fret 7, string 1 fret 5).
- **Over D7:** D (string 5 fret 5, string 3 fret 7).
- **Over E7:** E (string 5 fret 7, string 2 fret 5).

::fretboard[Box 1: notes]{scale="minor-pentatonic" root="A" box="1" labels="notes"}

## The solo

Bars 1–2 are a phrase: a bend, a short run, a long note. Bar 3 is a variant of it and bars 7–8 repeat it, so you have seven new bars to learn. Each phrase ends on a target note of the chord under it, and bars 11–12 end on E for the turnaround.

::exercise{id="twelve-bar-solo-a"}

::tip[The labels above the tab show the chord. Say "one, two…" up to twelve on every bar's first beat.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Getting lost in the form. Practise with the chord names in your head: A, A, A, A, D, D, A, A, E, D, A, E.
- Filling every beat. The long notes are half the solo; let them ring with vibrato.
- Bends out of tune. Check bar 1's bend against the A on string 1 two notes later.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the 12-bar solo at 80 BPM.
