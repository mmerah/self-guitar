A solo arrangement starts with the melody, alone and clear, on the top strings. Goal: the first eight bars of Amazing Grace in D at 80 BPM, played from a chord shape.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: sing the tune, then find it on strings 1–3 while holding a D shape.
- 4 minutes: the melody on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: bars 9–16 on your own: move each note of the library tab up a fifth.
- 2 minutes: choose a second tune for your own arrangement and test it against the three questions below.

## Choose the key

The library tab plays Amazing Grace in G, with the melody on strings 4 to 2. That leaves no room for a bass underneath. Before you play a note, ask three questions:

1. Does the melody fit on strings 1–3, mostly below fret 5?
2. Are the chord roots open bass strings (E, A or D)? Then the bass is free.
3. Do the melody notes fall inside the chord shapes?

In D the answers are yes: D and A are open bass strings, and only the G chord needs a fretted root (string 6, fret 3). Moving up a fifth turns G into D, B into F♯, D into A, A into E and E into B.

::song[The library version, melody in G]{id="amazing-grace" tempo=100}

## The melody from the chord shape

Hold a D chord. Three of the melody's notes are already under your fingers: A on string 3, D on string 2 and F♯ on string 1. E and B are open strings, and the high A is a pinky stretch to fret 5.

::chord{id="D"}

::fretboard[D major on strings 1–3]{scale="major" root="D" frets="0-5" strings="1-3" labels="notes"}

::exercise{id="amazing-grace-melody-d"}

Give every note its full length. The melody must sing on its own before anything goes under it.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Changing the tune to suit your fingers. Find fingering for the melody, never a melody for the fingering.
- A key with the melody too high. Above fret 7 the bass and melody drift too far apart for one hand.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the melody in D at 80 BPM.
