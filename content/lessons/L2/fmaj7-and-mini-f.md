The full F chord needs a barre across all six strings, which comes in Level 4. Until then, two smaller shapes do the job: Fmaj7, which is easy and pretty, and mini F, which is the real F sound on four strings.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute of C–Fmaj7 changes; then 1 minute of C–mini F.
- 3 minutes of the exercise from 52 BPM to 80.
- Review: 1 minute of C–G changes towards 50, the hardest of the common pairs.

## Fmaj7

**Ring** on fret 3 of the D string (4), **middle** on fret 2 of the G string (3), **index** on fret 1 of the B string (2). The high e rings open. Strum the four thinnest strings.

::chord{id="Fmaj7"}

Look at C: the index finger is already on fret 1 of the B string. To go from C to Fmaj7, the index stays and the ring and middle each move one string thinner. That's why C–Fmaj7 is one of the easiest changes there is.

::chords{ids="C,Fmaj7"}

## Mini F

Start from Fmaj7 and lay the index finger flat so it presses **both** the B and high e strings at fret 1. That small barre is the whole difference.

::chord{id="F"}

Pick the four thinnest strings one at a time. If the high e buzzes, roll the index slightly onto its bony side and bring the elbow in.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Strumming the low E and A strings: Fmaj7 and mini F start on the D string.
- Pressing the mini-F barre with the fingertip only, so the B or high e string is muted.
- Squeezing the neck for the barre. Pull back gently with the arm instead of pinching with the thumb.

:::

**Mastery check:** 40 changes in one minute between C and Fmaj7.
