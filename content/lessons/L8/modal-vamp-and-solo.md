A vamp is one or two chords on repeat; a modal solo makes the mode's colour note sing over it. Goal: a 16-bar D Dorian solo over your own recorded Dm7–G vamp.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the Dorian phrase on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: record the vamp below for 2 minutes at 80 BPM (a phone is fine).
- 5 minutes: solo over the recording, following the three rules.
- 1 minute: listen back to 16 bars. Write down one thing that worked.

## The vamp

Each mode has a signature vamp. Dorian's is i to IV: Dm7 to G major. The B in the G chord is the Dorian 6th, so the vamp itself sounds Dorian. Mixolydian's is I to ♭VII: D to C, where C is the ♭7.

```atex
\tempo 80
:4 (0.4 2.3 1.2 1.1){ch "Dm7"} (0.4 2.3 1.2 1.1) (0.4 2.3 1.2 1.1) (0.4 2.3 1.2 1.1) | (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2){ch "G"} (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2)
```

## Three rules for a modal solo

1. **Land on chord tones** on beat 1: D, F, A or C over Dm7; G, B or D over G.
2. **Feature the colour note.** Lean on B over Dm7: that one note says Dorian.
3. **Leave space.** Play two bars, rest two bars, answer yourself.

## Then Mixolydian

Record a D–C vamp, swap to D Mixolydian and make the C the star. Same rules, a brighter, bluesier mood.

::fretboard[D Mixolydian, frets 4–7]{scale="mixolydian" root="D" frets="4-7"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Running the scale up and down. A solo is phrases: a few notes, a rest, an answer.
- Playing B♭ over the vamp. That's D natural minor, and it clashes with the B in the G chord.
- Ending phrases on E or G over Dm7. They sound unfinished; end on D, F or A.

:::
