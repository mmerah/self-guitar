The open chords C, A, G, E and D are five shapes of one pattern. Slide each up the neck and you can play any major chord in five places. Goal: C in three shapes, then G in its G, E and D shapes at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: look at each shape of C below; find its root dots (the squares) first.
- 3 minutes: the C exercise from 52 BPM, picking each shape root to top.
- 4 minutes: the G exercise the same way, up to 80.
- Review: one full-neck note quiz round.

## Five shapes, one chord

Every shape is an open chord with its nut replaced by your index finger (or a barre). The root on string 6, 5 or 4 names the chord.

::fretboard[The C shape of C (open)]{caged="C" root="C"}

::fretboard[The A shape of C (barre at 3)]{caged="A" root="C"}

::fretboard[The G shape of C (from fret 5)]{caged="G" root="C"}

::fretboard[The E shape of C (barre at 8)]{caged="E" root="C"}

::fretboard[The D shape of C (from fret 10)]{caged="D" root="C"}

The order up the neck is always C, A, G, E, D, then C again an octave higher. Starting from another chord just starts the cycle at another letter: for G the order is G, E, D, C, A.

## Three that you can play today

The open C, the A shape and the E shape are the barre chords you know. Together they cover the neck from the nut to fret 10.

## A new root: G

Now start the cycle at G. The open G is the G shape, the E shape sits at fret 3, and the D shape at fret 5, with its root on string 4.
