Am uses exactly the same finger shape as E, moved one string towards the thin strings. Together with Em it gives you two minor chords and a first minor-key loop.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute of Am–E changes (the easy one), then 1 minute of Em–Am (the real challenge).
- 4 minutes of the exercise, from 46 BPM up to 70.
- Review: 1 minute of Em–E changes from the last lesson.

## Am

**Middle** on fret 2 of the D string (4), **ring** on fret 2 of the G string (3), **index** on fret 1 of the B string (2). Strum from the open A string (5); the low E stays silent.

::chord{id="Am"}

## One shape, two places

Play E, then keep the shape exactly as it is and move all three fingers one string towards the thin strings. That is Am. Going back is the same move towards the thick strings.

::chords{ids="E,Am"}

::tool[Am to E changes]{name="changes" a="Am" b="E"}

## Em ↔ Am

This one is a real hand move. From Em, the middle and ring fingers each shift one string thinner (A and D strings to D and G strings), and the index lands on fret 1 of the B string. Lift all three together and land them together.

::tool[Em to Am changes]{name="changes" a="Em" b="Am"}

## Play it

When you loop it, E pulls back to Am: the sound of a minor key.

::exercise{id="am-em-e-quarters"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Strumming the low E string on Am (start from string 5).
- The index finger on Am leaning on the high e string and muting it.
- Placing fingers one at a time. The whole shape moves as one block.

:::

**Mastery check:** 30 changes in one minute between Em and Am.
