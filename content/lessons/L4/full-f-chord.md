F at fret 1 is the hardest place for a barre: the strings are highest and the frets widest. Goal: a full six-string F inside C, Am, F, G at 76 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: build F from mini F, below, picking each string.
- 1 minute: C to F changes with the full barre.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 50 BPM up to 76.
- Review: 1 minute of the E-shape barre at fret 3 (G).

## Build it from mini F

You already play mini F: index flat on strings 1 and 2, middle on string 3, ring on string 4. Add one string at a time:

1. **Mini F** (four strings): index on fret 1 of strings 1–2.
2. **Five strings:** move the ring to fret 3 of string 5 and add the pinky on fret 3 of string 4.
3. **Six strings:** lay the index all the way across fret 1, so it frets string 6 as well.

::chord{id="F" voicing="1-3-3-2-1-1"}

If step 3 won't ring yet, play step 2 in songs and keep working on the full barre in practice. That five-string F sounds good too.

## Getting the low frets to ring

- Turn the guitar neck slightly up, and bring the elbow closer to your body: the index then lies flatter.
- Roll the index onto its outer edge, near the fret wire.
- If one string still buzzes, check the action of your guitar: a high nut makes fret 1 barres much harder. A guitar shop can lower it for little money.

## In a song

Wildwood Flower moves between C, F and G7. Play every F as the full barre.

::song[Wildwood Flower, with the full F]{id="wildwood-flower" tempo=80}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The index rests on the fret wire instead of just behind it: strings 1 and 2 go dead.
- Pressing harder and harder. Re-angle the index instead; strength comes slowly over weeks.
- Arriving late on F because the ring and pinky land one by one. Place them as a block.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of C, Am, F, G at 76 BPM.
