I, IV, V and vi are the four chords behind most folk and pop songs. Goal: I–V–vi–IV in G, C, D, A and E, with the barres in A and E at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: I–V–vi–IV in G and D, from 52 BPM.
- 1 minute: the same in C (C G Am F), with the full F.
- 4 minutes: A and E, the barre keys, from 52 BPM up to 80.
- 2 minutes: the ear quiz, any key.
- Review: 1 minute of D to Bm.

## Four orders, one family

The same four chords make different songs depending on the order:

- **I–V–vi–IV:** the modern pop loop.
- **I–vi–IV–V:** the 1950s doo-wop loop.
- **I–IV–V–I:** folk, country, hymns.
- **vi–IV–I–V:** the same loop starting on the minor chord.

Pick a key, then play all four orders. In G: G D Em C, G Em C D, G C D G, Em C G D.

## C

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Stopping between keys. Keep the beat: the last chord of one key leads straight into the first of the next.
- Playing F♯m as F♯ (major). vi is always minor.
- Slowing down for the barres only. Choose a tempo where the hardest bar is clean.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of I–V–vi–IV in A and E at 80 BPM.
