Five notes in one hand position: box 1 of A minor pentatonic is where most blues solos start. Goal: the run up and down at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: tap the dots below, low to high, and copy each one.
- 5 minutes: the run on the tempo ladder, from 64 BPM.
- 2 minutes: the ear quiz on 4ths, 5ths and octaves.
- Review: one pass of the 12-bar blues in A.

::exercise{id="a-minor-pentatonic-box-1"}

## What the notes are

The squares are A, the root. The other four notes are the ♭3, 4, 5 and ♭7 above it.

::fretboard[Box 1: intervals]{scale="minor-pentatonic" root="A" box="1" labels="intervals"}

## Hear the 4th and the 5th

The root, 4 and 5 are the strongest notes to end a phrase on. Train your ear to find them.

::ear{kind="intervals" set="P4,P5,P8" n=10}

::song[Try the box over a song in A minor]{id="joshua-fit-the-battle-of-jericho" tempo=80}

::tip[Keep the fingers hovering just above the strings: small moves are fast moves.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Playing every note with the index and ring. Frets 5 and 8 on strings 6, 2 and 1 need the pinky.
- Picking only down. Alternate down-up from the first note, even when it feels slower.
- Rushing the string changes. Slow down until every note is even.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the box-1 run at 80 BPM.
