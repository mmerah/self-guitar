Step two: one bass note under each bar, played by the thumb while the fingers keep the tune. You did this in Level 7 with a ready-made arrangement in C; now you choose the notes, in a key with open bass strings. Goal: melody and bass in D at 80 BPM, the melody still the loudest voice.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: only the bass notes on beat 1, humming the melody.
- 4 minutes: melody and bass on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: try the walking bass below in bars 3–4.
- 2 minutes: add bass notes to the first eight bars of your own tune.

## Which bass note?

Start with the root of each chord. The library sheet's chords in G become D, G, A and D7 in our key.

::chords{ids="D,G,A,D7"}

D and A are open strings; only G needs a fretted root (string 6, fret 3). That is why D beats the C of Level 7, where every root was fretted.

::exercise{id="amazing-grace-bass-d"}

## Beyond the root

Once the roots work, look for a bass line. Any chord tone can go in the bass: F♯ under a D chord makes D/F♯. In bars 3–4 the bass can step down from G to F♯ instead of leaping back to D: a bass that moves by step makes a line of its own.

```atex
\tempo 70
\ts (3 4)
\voice
:2 3.2{ch "G"} :4 0.2 | :2 2.3{ch "D/F#"} :4 2.3
\voice
:2 3.6{d} | :2 2.6{d}
```

## Balance

The thumb plays a touch softer than the fingers. Let the bass ring through the bar, but stop it when the chord changes: an open D ringing into the A chord muddies it. Touch the string with the thumb to damp it.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- A bass louder than the melody. Record yourself: can you still sing along to the tune?
- Open bass strings ringing into the next chord. Damp them with the thumb.
- Thumb and fingers drifting apart on beat 1. The bass and the melody note must sound as one pinch.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of melody and bass at 80 BPM.
