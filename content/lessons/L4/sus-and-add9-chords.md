Change one note of a chord you know and it gets a new colour: suspended, open or dreamy. Goal: D and A with their sus chords, and Cadd9, at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: each chord pair below, back and forth, listening for the change.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 52 BPM up to 80.
- Review: 1 minute of C to Fmaj7.

## sus4 and sus2

A major chord has a root, a 3rd and a 5th. A **sus** chord swaps the 3rd for a neighbour: the 4th (sus4) or the 2nd (sus2). Without its 3rd the chord is neither major nor minor, so it wants to move back.

- **Dsus4:** D, with the pinky on fret 3 of string 1.
- **Dsus2:** D with string 1 open.
- **Asus4:** A, with the ring finger moved to fret 3 of string 2. Or keep the A down and add the pinky there.
- **Asus2:** A with string 2 open.

::chords{ids="D,Dsus4,Dsus2"}

::chords{ids="A,Asus4,Asus2"}

Play D, then Dsus4, then D again: the sus4 pulls back home. That little move decorates countless songs.

## add9

**Cadd9** is C with the high G and D added on top: ring and pinky on fret 3 of strings 2 and 1. It shares those two fingers with the G shape that has ring and pinky on fret 3, so G to Cadd9 moves only two fingers.

::chord{id="Cadd9"}

## maj7

A **maj7** adds the note one fret below the root: soft and dreamy. You know Fmaj7. Two more open ones, Cmaj7 (C with string 2 open) and Gmaj7 (G with fret 2 on string 1):

```atex
\tempo 70
:2 (3.5 2.4 0.3 0.2 0.1) (3.5 2.4 0.3 1.2 0.1) | (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 2.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1)
```

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Holding the sus chord as long as the main one. It's a passing colour: one beat, then resolve.
- Lifting the whole hand for a sus chord. Only one finger moves.
- Strumming string 6 on Cadd9.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the sus exercise at 80 BPM.
