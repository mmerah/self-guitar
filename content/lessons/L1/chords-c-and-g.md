C and G spread the fingers over three frets, so they take a little longer to feel comfortable. They are worth it: together with D, Em and Am they cover a large share of folk and pop songs.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: pick each string of C and of G; fix any dull string.
- 1 minute of C–G changes. 10 is a normal first score.
- 3 minutes of the exercise, from 46 BPM up to 70.
- Review: 1 minute of A–D or Em–D changes.

## C

**Ring** on fret 3 of the A string (5), **middle** on fret 2 of the D string (4), **index** on fret 1 of the B string (2). The G and high e strings ring open; don't play the low E.

::chord{id="C"}

Compare it with Am: the index (B string, fret 1) and middle (D string, fret 2) are in exactly the same places. Only the ring finger moves, from fret 2 of the G string to fret 3 of the A string.

## G

**Middle** on fret 3 of the low E (6), **index** on fret 2 of the A string (5), **ring** on fret 3 of the high e (1). The four middle strings ring open.

::chord{id="G" voicing="3-2-0-0-0-3"}

::tip[The chord library also shows a four-finger G (3-2-0-0-3-3): the same middle and index, ring on fret 3 of the B string and pinky on fret 3 of the high e. You'll use it for G–D in the next lesson.]

## C ↔ G

This is a big move, so let the ring finger lead: it travels from fret 3 of string 5 across to fret 3 of string 1. The middle drops from string 4 to fret 3 of string 6, and the index from string 2 to fret 2 of string 5. Practise the move slowly in the air before you time it.

::tool[C to G changes]{name="changes" a="C" b="G"}

## Strum it

One bar of C, one bar of G, four down-strums each.

::exercise{id="c-g-quarters"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- On C, the ring finger touching the D string, or the middle finger muting the open G.
- On G, the index finger muting the open D string. Keep it arched.
- Wrist bent sharply. Bring the elbow in and the thumb lower on the neck for the stretch.

:::

**Mastery check:** 30 changes in one minute between C and G.
