You can already strum 16ths. Now make them groove: a loud backbeat on 2 and 4, light "ghost" strums in between, then mutes in place of the accents. Goal: the accented groove at 90 BPM, three clean passes.

::strum{pattern="D-DU DUDU D-DU DUDU" bpm=70 chord="Em"}

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: muted strings in 16ths at 70, accenting only beats 2 and 4.
- 4 minutes: the groove exercise from 58 BPM up to 90.
- 3 minutes: the muted version, from 58 BPM.
- Review: one pass of the L4 16th exercise at 70.

::tool[16th clicks at 90]{name="metronome" bpm=90 sub=4}

## Accents and ghost strums

The groove lives in the difference between loud and soft. Hit the down-strum on 2 and 4 hard, like a snare drum. Every other strum is a **ghost**: the pick barely brushes two or three strings, so you feel the 16ths more than you hear them.

::exercise{id="sixteenth-groove"}

## Muted strums

Now replace each accent with a muted strum (x in the tab): relax the fretting fingers so they still touch the strings, and the chord becomes a click.

::exercise{id="muted-sixteenths"}

::warning[Don't press harder to go faster. A tight grip on the pick or the neck is what stops a 16th groove at 70.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the accented groove at 90 BPM.
