Transcribing means working out music from a recording. Goal: a method that finds any simple melody, proved on an 8-bar tune you then check against its tab.

## How to practise this

- 6 minutes: one phrase of Auld Lang Syne (below), using the method, screen turned away.
- 2 minutes: write it down as tab with its rhythm, then compare with the library tab.
- 2 minutes: the interval quiz, mixed directions.

## The method

1. **Listen** to the phrase three times, without the guitar. Then sing it.
2. **Find home.** Sing the note the tune ends on and find it on string 6 or 5. That's usually the key.
3. **Find the first note.** Sing it, then search for it, one note at a time.
4. **Follow the shape.** Up or down? A step or a leap? The interval tells you how far.
5. **Find the rhythm.** Tap your foot along and notice which notes fall on the beat.
6. **Check.** Play along with the recording and fix what differs.

Loop two to four seconds at a time, and slow the recording down if your player can do it without changing the pitch.

## Sing first, then play

If you can sing it, you can find it. Singing stops you guessing on the fretboard.

## Intervals: your tape measure

::ear{kind="intervals" set="m2,M2,m3,M3,P4,TT,P5,m6,M6,m7,M7,P8" dir="mixed" n=20}

For a first try, transcribe the opening of a library tune with the screen turned away, then compare your version with the tab.

::song[Listen, don't look: Auld Lang Syne]{id="auld-lang-syne" tempo=80}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Hunting on the guitar before singing. Singing first is faster.
- Taking on too much. One phrase, finished and checked, beats a page of guesses.
- Getting the notes right but the rhythm wrong. Tap along before you write.

:::
