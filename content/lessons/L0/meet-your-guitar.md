Before the first note, get to know the instrument and find a relaxed, stable way to hold it.

## How to practise this

- Touch and name each part: headstock, tuners, nut, frets, neck, body, sound hole, bridge.
- Sit with the guitar on your right leg, body against your chest. Let go of the neck: it stays put.
- Fretting hand: thumb behind the neck, fingertips just behind a fret. Place and lift it 5 times.

## The parts you'll hear about

- **Headstock** and **tuners**: the top end, where you tune each string.
- **Nut**: the small slotted bar where the strings leave the headstock.
- **Neck** and **frets**: the metal strips across the neck. "Fret 3" means the space just behind the third strip.
- **Body**, **sound hole** and **bridge**: the big wooden box that makes the sound, and the bar that anchors the strings.

The strings are numbered from the thinnest (**1**, high e) to the thickest (**6**, low E). String 1 is closest to the floor when you hold the guitar.

## Sitting posture

1. Sit near the front of a chair without arms, feet flat.
2. Rest the waist of the guitar on your right leg (left leg if you play left-handed).
3. Let the body lean back slightly against your chest, so you can just see the top edge of the neck.
4. Your strumming forearm rests on the top edge of the body and holds the guitar in place.

:::tip
Your fretting hand should not carry any weight. Let go of the neck: if the guitar stays put, your posture is right.
:::

## The fretting hand

Place your thumb behind the neck, roughly opposite your middle finger. Curve your fingers so the **tips** come down on the strings, just behind a fret wire. Keep your wrist loose and your shoulder low.

::warning[Fingertips may feel sore for the first couple of weeks. Short daily sessions build calluses faster than one long one.]
