"The House of the Rising Sun" is a traditional American folk song, and its rolling 6/8 arpeggio is a classic fingerstyle workout. It combines p-i-m-a, five open chords and mini F.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the hard changes on their own, D to F and C to E, without picking.
- 5 minutes: the exercise, from 44 up to 68 on the ladder. Keep the arpeggio even; don't pause before a change.
- Play it quietly. This song sounds best with a soft, flowing touch.
- Review: 2 minutes of the p-i-m-i exercise on C, Am, Dm, G.

## The pattern

Every bar is one chord and six even notes: the **thumb** plays the bass, then **i-m-a-m-i** play strings **3-2-1-2-3**.

```atex
\tempo 60
\ts (6 8)
:8 0.5{ch "Am"} 2.3 1.2 0.1 1.2 2.3
```

The verse is Am – C – D – F – Am – C – E – E, then the second half resolves back to Am.

::chords{ids="Am,C,D,F,E"}

Thumb strings: **5** for Am and C, **4** for D and F, **6** for E. The fingers stay on strings 3, 2 and 1 throughout.

## 6/8 and the tempo numbers

6/8 has six eighth notes per bar, felt as two big beats: "**1** 2 3 **4** 5 6". The thumb falls on 1; the high note (a, string 1) falls on 4. Let that note sing.

The ladder counts **quarter notes**, as every exercise in the app does. In 6/8 a quarter note is two eighths, so 68 on the ladder means 136 eighth notes a minute. The metronome link below clicks every **eighth note**, so its number is double the ladder's: it starts at 88, which matches 44 on the ladder.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Hitting string 5 on D or F. Both start from the D string (4).
- The mini-F barre not covering the high e, which the a finger plays.
- Rushing beat 4 because it's the high note. Every eighth note is the same length.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the exercise at 68 on the ladder (136 eighth notes a minute).
