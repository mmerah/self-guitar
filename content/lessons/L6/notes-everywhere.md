With strings 6 and 5 known, the other four come from shapes you already have. Goal: find every position of a note, on all six strings, in seconds.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the "every C" quiz, then the same for G.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 40 BPM, one note on every string per bar.
- 3 minutes: full-neck quiz rounds until one takes under 60 s.
- Review: one octave-shape bar from the last lesson.

## Three routes to any note

- **Strings 6 and 1** carry the same names at the same frets, two octaves apart.
- **String 4** is string 6's octave shape: two strings up, two frets up.
- **String 3** is string 5's octave shape (two frets up); **string 2** is string 4's (three frets up, past the B-string kink).

::fretboard[Every G on the neck]{note="G" labels="notes"}

Pick one note a day. Find it on string 6, then follow the octave shapes up and across until you have all of them below fret 12.

::fretboard{quiz="note-C"}

::exercise{id="every-string-c-g-d-a"}

::warning[Say the note before you play it. Hunting with the ear first trains the ear, not the map.]

## The full-neck quiz

Each round names three notes; tap every position of each, on all six strings (frets 0–11).

::fretboard{quiz="all"}

::tip[Stuck? Find the note on string 6 first and work outward with octave shapes.]

**Mastery check:** a full-neck round in 60 seconds or less, at most one miss per ten taps.
