Hear two notes and name the distance between them, from a half step to an octave, up, down or together. Then hear a note and find it on the neck.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: the rising quiz, then the falling one.
- 3 minutes: the mixed quiz with all twelve intervals.
- 2 minutes: intervals played together.
- 2 minutes: play-back (needs a microphone), or sing a note and find it on string 6.
- Review: one quiz round on the full neck.

## Anchors

Tie each interval to a sound you already know, and play it on one string to check: the number of frets is the number of half steps.

- **m2** (1 fret): tense, a horror-film creep. **M2** (2): the first two notes of a scale.
- **m3** (3) and **M3** (4): the bottom of a minor and a major chord.
- **P4** (5): a bugle call, open and bright. **TT** (6): halfway, restless.
- **P5** (7): a power chord. **m6** (8) and **M6** (9): wide and sweet.
- **m7** (10): the bluesy 7th. **M7** (11): one fret short of the octave. **P8** (12): the same note, higher.

## Together

Played as one sound, small intervals beat and rub, 3rds and 6ths blend, and 4ths, 5ths and octaves sound hollow.

::ear{kind="intervals" set="m3,M3,P4,P5,m6,M6,P8" dir="harm" n=10}

## Play it back

Hear a note, hum it, then find it on the neck. Start on string 6 below fret 12 and slide until the pitch matches.

::ear{kind="playback"}

::warning[Don't guess fast. Sing the two notes in your head first; a wrong guess at speed only teaches the wrong answer.]

**Mastery check:** your last 24 interval answers, all twelve intervals heard, at least 80 % right.
