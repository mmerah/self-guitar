The level's target: 16 bars from a recording into tab, melody and chords. Goal: a complete, playable transcription in a week of short sessions.

## How to practise this

Ten minutes a day for five days:

- Day 1: choose the recording, loop it and map the form.
- Days 2 and 3: the melody, eight bars a day.
- Day 4: the chords.
- Day 5: check the rhythm, write a clean tab and play along.

## Choosing the piece

Pick a recording you love with a clear melody, a moderate tempo and few instruments: a folk singer with a guitar, a fiddle tune, a solo guitarist. Keep transcriptions of copyrighted recordings for your own study.

## Map the form first

Before any notes, count the bars. Sixteen bars are usually four phrases of four. Label them (A A B A, for example), and mark where the chords change. Now you know where every note will go.

## Write it down

Use paper with bar lines, a tab editor, or plain text. Write the rhythm, not just the frets: a tab without rhythm can't be played by someone who hasn't heard the song. The tabs in this app are text, too:

```atex
\tempo 80
:4 0.3 2.3 :8 0.2 1.2 :4 0.2 | :2 2.3 0.3
```

## Check

Play along with the recording at full speed. Listen for wrong notes, wrong octaves and wrong rhythms, and fix one at a time.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- A piece that's too hard. Fast solos and dense bands are for later; start simple.
- Polishing bar 1 for an hour. Map the whole thing roughly, then refine.
- Trusting a tab you found online. Your ear is the point of this lesson.

:::
