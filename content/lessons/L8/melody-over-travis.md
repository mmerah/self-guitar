In basic Travis the fingers fill the gaps. Now they sing a tune on top of the thumb. Goal: a 4-bar melody over G, C, D7 and G at 80 BPM, with the thumb never wavering.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the thumb alone through all four bars, until it runs without thought.
- 2 minutes: hum the melody, then play it alone without the thumb.
- 5 minutes: both together on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- Review: 1 minute of the basic Travis pattern on C, G and Am.

## Two layers

Think of the thumb as a clock and the melody as a voice. A melody note on the beat is a pinch with the thumb. A melody note between beats is a finger alone, exactly halfway to the next thumb note.

The thumb layer of bar 1, then the melody layer:

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 3.6 0.4 3.6 0.4 | :4 3.1 :8 r 0.1 :4 3.2 :8 r 0.2
```

Notes on the "and" of 2 and 4 fall between the thumb's beats, so the melody answers the bass. They become a **push** only when tied over the next beat, so that no new note sounds with the thumb: that syncopation is what makes Travis melodies swing.

## Let the melody sing

- Pluck melody notes a little louder than the thumb. Rest the edge of the picking hand lightly on the bass strings for a softer, thumpy bass.
- Keep each melody finger down for the note's full length.
- On G, use the 3-2-0-0-3-3 shape: the pinky is already on the first melody note.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The thumb stopping when a melody note falls between beats. Go back to 52 BPM and say "thumb" on every beat.
- Melody notes landing with the thumb when they should come after it.
- Fill notes drowning the tune. Fewer, quieter fills make a clearer melody.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the Travis melody at 80 BPM.
