In an open tuning the six open strings play a major chord, so one flat finger across any fret plays another. Open D is big and ringing; open G is the tuning of slide and of much folk blues. Goal: I, IV and V in both, at 90 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 4 minutes: open D, tune with the tuner, then the barre exercise from 58 BPM.
- 4 minutes: open G, tune, then the slide exercise from 58 BPM.
- 1 minute: tune back to standard and check every string.
- Review: the DADGAD drone pattern, once.

## Open D: D A D F♯ A D

From standard, four strings go down: 6, 2 and 1 a whole step (E to D, B to A, E to D) and string 3 a half step (G to F♯).

::tool[Tuner in open D]{name="tuner" tuning="open-d"}

The open strings are D major. Barre fret 5 for G, fret 7 for A, fret 12 for D an octave up: the I, IV and V of D.

::exercise{id="open-d-barres"}

## Open G: D G D G B D

Three strings go down a whole step: 6 (E to D), 5 (A to G) and 1 (E to D). Strings 4, 3 and 2 stay.

::tool[Tuner in open G]{name="tuner" tuning="open-g"}

The root is on string 5, and the thumb alternates between strings 5 and 4. Barre 5 is C, barre 7 is D. A slide or a sliding finger between these frets is the classic sound.

::exercise{id="open-g-slide"}

::warning[Your chord shapes from standard tuning don't work here: in open tunings, think in barres and single notes.]

::tip[A capo works as usual: open G with a capo at 2 is open A.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the open-G exercise at 90 BPM.
