Same 12-bar form, new key, new feel. In E the form uses E7, A7 and B7, and instead of straight eighths it **shuffles**: each beat is split long-short, giving that rolling blues swing.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes on E7 alone, shuffling along with the exercise playback, until the long-short feel is natural.
- The exercise from 52 BPM up to 80, counting bars.
- Bonus: play the 12-bar in A from the last lesson with a shuffle.
- Review: 1 minute of E7–B7 changes.

## The form in E

- **Line 1:** E7 – E7 – E7 – E7
- **Line 2:** A7 – A7 – E7 – E7
- **Line 3:** B7 – A7 – E7 – B7

It's the same 12-bar form as in A, in a new key: I is E7, IV is A7, V is B7.

::chords{ids="E7,A7,B7"}

## The shuffle feel

Divide each beat into three and strum on the first and third parts: "**1** - a **2** - a". Down on the beat (long), up on the "a" just before the next beat (short). Say "**hump**-ty **dump**-ty" in time and you've got it.

The exercise below is played with a swing feel, so it is your reference for the sound. Listen to one pass before you play.

::exercise{id="twelve-bar-shuffle-e"}

## Listen to a shuffle

The library has a single-note **12-bar shuffle in E** on the bass strings. Listen to its swing, then strum the chords along with it. You'll learn to play that riff in the boogie lesson.

- [12-Bar Blues Shuffle in E](/play/blues-in-e-shuffle)

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Swinging some beats and playing others straight. The feel stays the same all the way through.
- Making the up-strum too heavy. In a shuffle it's a short, light flick.
- B7 changes arriving late. Practise E7–B7 with the anchor finger.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the shuffle exercise at 80 BPM.
