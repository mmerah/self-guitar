Step three: fills where the melody holds, and an ending. Then the real test, your own arrangement from start to finish. Goal: Amazing Grace complete, and a second tune arranged by you.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: the full arrangement on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 5 minutes: your own tune, steps 1–3, eight bars at a time.
- 2 minutes: record one pass of whatever is finished.

## Where fills go

Fills follow three rules:

1. Only while the melody holds a note. In this tune that is beat 2 of almost every bar.
2. Below the melody, never above it.
3. A note of the current chord: A over D, C over D7, G over G, A or C♯ over A.

Where the melody itself sits on string 3 (bars 4 and 12), there is no room below it, so the bass rings alone.

## An ending

Arrangers often add a tag after the last melody note. Here the bass walks up A, B, C♯ into a final D chord. Another option: use the last four bars, played softer, as an intro.

## Your own arrangement

Pick a tune you can sing, then go through the steps: key, melody on top, bass, fills, an intro or ending, a recording. These library tunes work well:

::song[The Water Is Wide]{id="water-is-wide" tempo=100}

::song[Skye Boat Song]{id="skye-boat-song" tempo=100}

::song[Down by the Salley Gardens]{id="down-by-the-salley-gardens" tempo=100}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Too many fills. If every gap is filled, nothing stands out. Leave some held notes alone.
- Fills that cover the melody. Play them softer than the tune.
- Stopping at "nearly done". Write it down and record it, even with rough spots.

:::
