Classical players pluck in two ways: the rest stroke for a full, singing melody and the free stroke for everything else. Goal: a scale in clean, even rest strokes at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: free strokes with i and m on the open string 1, then rest strokes; listen for the difference.
- 5 minutes: the rest-stroke scale from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: the same scale in free strokes, as loud as you can without buzzing.
- Review: a p-i-m-a bar on C from Level 3.

## Rest stroke (apoyando)

The finger plucks through the string and comes to rest on the next, thicker string. All the energy goes into the string, so the note is loud and round. Use it for melodies and scales. The thumb's rest stroke lands on the next thinner string.

## Free stroke (tirando)

The finger plucks and curls up into the palm, clearing the next string. It is lighter, and the only stroke that lets the lower strings keep ringing: arpeggios and chords need it.

## Planting and alternation

Place the fingertip on the string before you play it: that is **planting**. The note starts exactly when you push through, and you can't miss the string. In a scale, plant one finger while the other plays.

Alternate i and m for every note, never i-i or m-m, even when you change strings.

## Nails and tone

The string should leave from the nail's edge after touching the fingertip. Keep nails short, just past the fingertip, shaped in a smooth curve and polished with fine paper. Without nails, use the flesh at the left side of the tip (right-handed players).

Pluck over the sound-hole edge for a warm tone. Nearer the bridge it gets thin and bright, over the neck soft and round.
