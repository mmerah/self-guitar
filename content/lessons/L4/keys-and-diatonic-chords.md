A key is a family of seven notes, and each note carries a chord. Goal: name the chords of G, C, D, A and E major without looking.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: play the G major scale on string 6, saying the steps (whole, whole, half…).
- 4 minutes: write out the chords of each key below from memory, then check.
- 2 minutes: the ear quiz on I, IV, V and vi.
- Review: 1 minute of the full F.

## The major scale

Every major scale has the same steps: **whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half** (a whole step is two frets, a half step one). From G on string 6:

::fretboard[G major along string 6]{scale="major" root="G" strings="6" frets="3-15" labels="intervals"}

G A B C D E F♯ G. The F♯ is what makes it G major: the key of G has one sharp.

## Chords from the scale

Stack every other scale note on each step and you get seven chords, numbered I to vii in Roman numerals. The pattern of qualities is the same in every major key:

- **I, IV, V:** major (upper case).
- **ii, iii, vi:** minor (lower case).
- **vii°:** diminished, rarely used in folk and pop.

## The five keys

- **C:** C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, B°
- **G:** G, Am, Bm, C, D, Em, F♯°
- **D:** D, Em, F♯m, G, A, Bm, C♯°
- **A:** A, Bm, C♯m, D, E, F♯m, G♯°
- **E:** E, F♯m, G♯m, A, B, C♯m, D♯°

Now you know why Bm turns up in songs in D and G, and why F♯m and C♯m need barres in A and E.

::chords{ids="G,Am,Bm,C,D,Em"}

## Hear the numbers

Each chord has a job. I is home, V pulls home, IV lifts, vi is the sad cousin of I. Name what you hear:

::ear{kind="progressions" set="I,IV,V,vi" key="G" n=10}

::tip[Shortcut: the vi chord is three frets below the I. G to E, C to A, D to B.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Forgetting the sharps: in D major the iii chord is F♯m, not Fm.
- Counting the scale from 0. The root is 1, so the 5th of G is D.
- Mixing up vi and IV by ear. vi sounds minor; IV sounds major and bright.

:::

**Mastery check:** tick the three statements when they're true for you.
