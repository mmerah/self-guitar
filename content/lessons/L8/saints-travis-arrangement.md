Put it to work on a real song: the first half of When the Saints Go Marching In, melody and bass on one guitar. Goal: the 8-bar solo at 90 BPM, then the second half arranged by you.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: sing the melody with the chord sheet below.
- 2 minutes: bars 1–2 and 7–8 alone, slowly. They hold all the moves.
- 5 minutes: the whole solo on the tempo ladder, from 58 BPM.
- 2 minutes: start on bars 9–16 yourself (see the end of the lesson).

## How the arrangement is built

1. **Key:** G. The thumb gets G on string 6 and open D on string 4; the melody fits on strings 1–3.
2. **Melody:** D (held) | G B C | D (held) | G B C | D B | G B | A (held) | A, then G B C.
3. **Pinches:** every melody note on a beat is pinched with the thumb's bass note.
4. **Fills:** where the melody holds a long note, i and m fill the "ands" on strings 3 and 2, as in basic Travis.
5. **D7:** the thumb moves to strings 4 and 5, and the melody A sits under the middle finger.

The melody alone, bars 1–2:

```atex
\tempo 90
:1 3.2 | :4 r 0.3 0.2 1.2
```

Beats 2–4 of bar 8 are "Oh when the", the pickup into "saints". That is why the solo loops so naturally: to start, play just those three notes, then bar 1.

## Your turn: bars 9–16

The second half ("Oh Lord, I want to be in that number…") uses G, C and D. Work out its melody by ear on strings 1–3, then follow the five steps above. For C, the thumb plays strings 5 and 4.

::song[The chord sheet, all 16 bars]{id="when-the-saints-go-marching-in" tempo=100}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Losing the melody under the fills. If a listener can't sing along, play fewer fills.
- Holding the full G chord in bar 2. Lift the ring finger so the open B rings, and use the index for C.
- Stopping the thumb for the pickup in bar 8. The bass keeps going on every beat.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the Saints solo at 90 BPM.
