An acoustic guitar is also a drum. Goal: a kick, a snare and a thumb-slap backbeat, landing exactly on the click at 90 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: each sound alone, slowly, until ten in a row sound the same.
- 3 minutes: the kick-and-snare pulse on the tempo ladder, from 58 BPM.
- 4 minutes: the thumb-slap groove on Em and C, from 58 BPM.
- 1 minute: play the groove without the click, then turn it back on. Did you drift?

## Three sounds and how they are written

- **Kick (K):** let the heel of your picking hand drop onto the top right beside string 6, so the palm lands on the string too. You get a low thud and a damped string.
- **Snare (S):** the flat of i, m and a slaps strings 1–4 down onto the end of the fretboard. A short, dry crack.
- **Thumb slap:** turn the forearm so the side of the thumb strikes strings 6–4 against the frets. A crack with a bit of pitch, and the bass stops ringing.

In the tab an **x** is a dead note: a hit with no pitch. The K above the x on string 6 is the kick; the slap mark (S) on a stack of x's is a slap, and its strings tell you which one.

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 x.6{txt "K"} (x.4 x.3 x.2 x.1){s} (x.6 x.5 x.4){s} r
```

The sound comes from speed and weight, not force. Let the hand fall from 2–3 cm and stay on the strings for a moment: that silence is part of the hit.

## The thumb slap as a backbeat

In fingerstyle, the thumb already plays on every beat. Now it takes turns: a bass note on 1 and 3, a slap on 2 and 4, like a drummer's kick and snare. The fingers keep plucking between.

The move is a small rotation of the forearm, as if turning a door handle. The thumb lands flat across strings 6–4, close to the end of the fretboard.

::exercise{id="thumb-slap-em-c"}

::tool[Metronome at 58 BPM]{name="metronome" bpm=58}

::tip[Take off rings and watches, and keep hits away from the edge of the soundhole and any pickguard edge.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Hitting hard. Loud hits drift ahead of the beat and hurt your hand and the guitar.
- A slap that rings: leave the thumb on the strings until the next note.
- Rushing into the slap. A percussive hit must land on the click, just like a note.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the thumb-slap groove at 90 BPM.
