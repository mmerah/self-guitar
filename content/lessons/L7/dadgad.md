DADGAD retunes three strings so the open guitar rings a D chord without a 3rd: neither major nor minor. It's the sound of much Celtic and modern acoustic music. Goal: tune to it and play a ringing drone pattern at 90 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: tune down to DADGAD with the tuner.
- 5 minutes: the drone pattern from 58 BPM up to 90.
- 1 minute: improvise with the open strings and frets 0, 2 and 5 on the top strings.
- 1 minute: tune back to standard.
- Review: fret-12 harmonics on all six strings.

## Retuning

Three strings go down a whole step (two frets' worth); the other three stay:

- String 6: E down to **D**.
- String 2: B down to **A**.
- String 1: E down to **D**.

::tool[Tuner in DADGAD]{name="tuner" tuning="dadgad"}

Check with harmonics: the fret-12 harmonic on string 6 should match the open string 4, and string 1 open should match it an octave higher.

## Shapes

The open strings give D, A and G: a Dsus4. Fret string 3 at 2 for D5, a pure fifth-and-root chord. Fret string 5 at 2 and the G on string 3 becomes the root of a G chord over the D drone. Most DADGAD playing keeps the open strings ringing under a few fretted notes.

::exercise{id="dadgad-drone"}

::warning[Always tune down to these notes, never up: the D above E on string 6 is ten frets higher and could snap the string.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the drone pattern at 90 BPM.
