Jazz harmony is built from four-note chords. Goal: play maj7, 7, m7 and m7b5 on one root, know the 6, 9, 11 and 13 sounds, and tell maj7, m7 and 7 apart by ear.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the four chords on C, saying each formula out loud.
- 3 minutes: the exercise on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 3 minutes: the ear quiz, two rounds.
- 2 minutes: the 6, 9, 11 and 13 shapes, one strum each. Listen to how each colours the chord.

## Four sevenths

A seventh chord is a triad with one more third on top. Change one note at a time and you walk through all four:

- **maj7:** 1 3 5 7. Cmaj7 = C E G B. Dreamy.
- **7 (dominant):** 1 3 5 ♭7. C7 = C E G B♭. Bluesy; it wants to move.
- **m7:** 1 ♭3 5 ♭7. Cm7 = C E♭ G B♭. Soft and mellow.
- **m7b5 (half-diminished):** 1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7. Cm7b5 = C E♭ G♭ B♭. Dark and restless.

## 6ths and extensions

Keep stacking thirds above the 7th and you get the extensions: the **9** (the 2nd an octave up), the **11** (the 4th) and the **13** (the 6th). A guitar can't hold all seven notes, so leave out the 5th first, then the root. Keep the 3rd and 7th: they say what kind of chord it is.

A **6** chord is different: a major triad plus the 6th, no 7th.

- **C6** x-3-2-2-1-0: C E A C E.
- **C9** x-3-2-3-3-3: C E B♭ D G. A C7 with the 9th.
- **Am11** x-0-0-0-1-0: A D G C E. The 11th is D; the 9th is left out.
- **G13** 3-x-3-4-5-x: G F B E. The 13th, E, on top; no 5th or 9th.

## Hear the difference

- **maj7** shimmers: the 7th sits a half step under the root.
- **7** has a sting: the 3rd and ♭7 are a tritone apart, and that tension wants to resolve.
- **m7** is soft, like minor with the edges rounded off.
- **m7b5** is darker still: a diminished triad with the ♭7. Learn it by playing it; the quiz sticks to the three common sevenths.

::ear{kind="chords" set="maj7,m7,7" n=10}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Calling C7 "C major 7". C7 has B♭; Cmaj7 has B.
- Mixing up m7b5 and dim7. Both have ♭3 and ♭5, but m7b5 has the ♭7 (B♭ over C) and dim7 the double-flat 7 (A).
- Letting stray open strings ring under movable shapes. Mute them with the fretting hand.

:::
