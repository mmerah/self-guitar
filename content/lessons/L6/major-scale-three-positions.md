One position of a scale is a four-fret box the hand can cover without moving. Learn G major in three of them and you can play a melody anywhere from fret 2 to fret 12.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: position 1 over two octaves, from 64 BPM.
- 5 minutes: the three-position exercise, from 64 BPM up to 100.
- 1 minute: play the open G string and sing "do"; climb position 1 singing "re mi fa…".
- Review: the C major scale in first position, once.

## Position 1: root on string 6

Index at fret 2, one finger per fret. The root G is under the middle finger on strings 6 and 1 (fret 3), and under the pinky on string 4 (fret 5).

::fretboard[G major, position 1]{scale="major" root="G" frets="2-5" labels="fingers"}

::exercise{id="g-major-position-1"}

## Positions 2 and 3: root on string 5

Position 2 puts the index at fret 7: the root is under the pinky on string 5 (fret 10) and the middle finger on string 2 (fret 8). Position 3 moves up two frets: index at 9, the root under the middle finger on string 5 and the pinky on string 3.

::fretboard[G major, position 2]{scale="major" root="G" frets="7-10" labels="fingers"}

::fretboard[G major, position 3]{scale="major" root="G" frets="9-12" labels="intervals"}

The positions overlap: the G at fret 10, string 5, belongs to both 2 and 3. Shift on that note.

::exercise{id="g-major-three-positions"}

::warning[Keep the hand in its position. If a finger reaches outside its fret, the scale shape is wrong or you've drifted.]

::tip[To play in another key, move all three positions so the root lands on that note: A major is two frets higher.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the three-position exercise at 100 BPM.
