16th notes split each beat into four: "1 e & a". Goal: two 16th patterns on Em, C, G and D at 70 BPM, three clean passes.

::strum{pattern="D-DU D-DU D-DU D-DU" bpm=60 chord="Em"}

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: count "1 e & a 2 e & a" aloud with the metronome at 60, four clicks a beat.
- 2 minutes: the hand down-up in 16ths on muted strings, saying the count.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 46 BPM up to 70.
- Review: 1 minute of D – DU – UDU on G.

## The count and the hand

Downs land on **1** and **&**; ups land on **e** and **a**. The hand never stops: it moves down-up four times a beat, and you choose when it touches the strings.

::tool[Metronome with 16ths]{name="metronome" bpm=60 sub=4}

## Pattern 1: "1 & a"

The strum at the top: down on the beat, down on the "&", up on the "a". The up on "e" misses. It's a gallop: **long, short-short**.

## Pattern 2: "1 … a"

Down on the beat, then only the up on the "a", just before the next beat. The hand still swings through "e" and "&" without touching. It sounds like a skip: **loooong-short**.

::strum{pattern="D--U D--U D--U D--U" bpm=60 chord="C"}

::exercise{id="sixteenth-strums-em-c-g-d"}

## In a song

Loch Lomond on G, C, D and Em: pattern 1 on the verse, pattern 2 on the chorus. The song's own strum is in 8ths; play your 16ths over its click.

::song[Loch Lomond with 16ths]{id="loch-lomond" tempo=80}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Stopping the hand on missed strums. The motion carries the time; keep it going.
- Heavy up-strums. Ups touch just the top two or three strings.
- Playing pattern 2 as dotted 8ths with no swing of the hand. Feel all four 16ths, even the silent ones.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the 16th exercise at 70 BPM.
