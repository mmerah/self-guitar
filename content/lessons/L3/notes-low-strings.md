The bass strings carry the roots of your chords and the low half of many melodies. Their first-position notes follow the same rules as the thin strings.

## How to practise this

- Name each note as you play it, out loud.
- The exercise from 52 BPM up to 80, with the thumb (p) or a pick.
- Quiz: hold a chord, find its root on string 6 or 5 and name it.
- Review: 1 minute of the notes on strings 1–3.

## The notes

One finger per fret again: index for fret 1, middle for fret 2, ring for fret 3.

- **String 6 (E):** open = **E**, fret 1 = **F**, fret 3 = **G**
- **String 5 (A):** open = **A**, fret 2 = **B**, fret 3 = **C**
- **String 4 (D):** open = **D**, fret 2 = **E**, fret 3 = **F**

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 0.6 1.6 3.6 0.5 | 2.5 3.5 0.4 2.4 | :2 3.4 r
```

Spot the familiar ones: fret 3 on string 5 is the root of your C chord, fret 3 on string 6 is the root of G.

## Play a melody

**Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star** in the library sits on strings 3–5: C on string 5, D, E and F on string 4, then G and A on string 3. Read it slowly, naming each note.

- [Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star](/play/twinkle-twinkle)

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Confusing string 6 (thickest) with string 1. Count from the bottom up: 1 is the thinnest.
- B on string 5 at fret 1. It's fret 2; fret 1 would be B♭.
- Letting the open bass strings ring into the next note. Mute with the thumb if it gets muddy.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the exercise at 80 BPM.
