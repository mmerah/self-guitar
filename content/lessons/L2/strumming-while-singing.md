Singing while you play feels like patting your head and rubbing your tummy at first. The trick is to add one layer at a time, so that the strumming runs on its own before your voice joins in.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: strum the song's chords with a simple pattern, without singing, until you could do it while chatting.
- 2 minutes: keep strumming and **speak** the words in rhythm.
- 3 minutes: sing. Quietly is fine. If it falls apart, go back to speaking.
- Review: 2 minutes of the [Drunken Sailor exercise](/learn/first-song-drunken-sailor).

## Step 1: strumming on autopilot

Pick a song whose chords you can change without thinking: **Drunken Sailor** (Em, D) or **Twinkle, Twinkle** (G, C, D). Use four down-strums a bar. Your strumming should be so steady that you could look away or talk.

::strum{pattern="D - D - D - D -" bpm=70 chord="Em"}

## Step 2: speak the words

Say the lyrics in rhythm, like a chant, while you strum. Notice which words land **on** a strum: in Drunken Sailor, "**What** shall we **do** with a **drunk**-en **sai**-lor" puts four words on the four beats. Those are your anchor points.

## Step 3: sing

Now sing the same words. Your voice and hands will want to sync up and stop together; let them be independent. The hand keeps the beat no matter what the voice does.

::exercise{id="drunken-sailor-strum"}

## Then add a pattern

When down-strums feel easy, switch to D – DU – UDU, and use the same three steps again: strum alone, speak, sing.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Stopping the strum when you forget a word. Hum or skip it; the hand keeps going.
- Strumming in time with the melody's syllables instead of the beat.
- Starting with a song you can't yet play without looking. Pick the easiest one first.

:::
