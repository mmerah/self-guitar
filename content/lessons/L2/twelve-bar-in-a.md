The 12-bar blues is a chord sequence behind a century of blues, rock'n'roll and folk: twelve bars, three chords, always in the same order. The goal is to play it three times through without getting lost.

## How to practise this

- First play the form with one down-strum per bar, counting the bars aloud.
- Then the exercise from 52 BPM up to 80.
- Play three choruses (3 × 12 bars) in a row without getting lost.
- Review: 1 minute of E7–B7 changes.

## The form

The three chords are built on the 1st, 4th and 5th notes of the key, written **I**, **IV** and **V**. In A they are A7, D7 and E7. Four bars per line:

- **Line 1:** A7 – A7 – A7 – A7 (I)
- **Line 2:** D7 – D7 – A7 – A7 (IV, back to I)
- **Line 3:** E7 – D7 – A7 – E7 (V, IV, I, and V to turn around)

The last bar, E7, is the **turnaround**: it pulls you back to the top. On the very last time through, end on A7 instead.

## D7

**Middle** on fret 2 of the G string, **index** on fret 1 of the B string, **ring** on fret 2 of the high e. Four strings, like D.

::chords{ids="A7,D7,E7"}

## Count the bars

Counting is the whole trick. Say the bar number on every beat 1: "**One**, 2, 3, 4, **Two**, 2, 3, 4…" up to twelve.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Losing count in line 3 and going back to A7 too early.
- Forgetting the turnaround bar and staying on A7 for bar 12.
- Hurrying the D7 change. Use the open-string trick on the "&" of 4.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the 12-bar exercise at 80 BPM.
