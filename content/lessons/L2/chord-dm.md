Dm completes the three main chords of the key of A minor: Am and Dm are minor, and E is major (that major E is what pulls the music back to Am). Dm is a small four-string shape, close to D.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute of Am–Dm changes daily. Expect around 20 at first; 40 is the goal.
- 3 minutes of the exercise from 52 BPM up to 80.
- Review: 1 minute of a Level 1 pair (C–G or G–D), pushing it towards 50.

## Dm

**Middle** on fret 2 of the G string (3), **ring** on fret 3 of the B string (2), **index** on fret 1 of the high e (1). Strum only the four thinnest strings, from the open D.

::chord{id="Dm"}

Compare it with D: the ring finger stays on fret 3 of the B string. Only the other two fingers swap places.

::chords{ids="D,Dm"}

## Change targets: 40, then 50

In Level 1 the target was 30 changes a minute. For a brand-new pair like Am–Dm the first goal is **40**; pairs you already know should reach **50**, which is fast enough for nearly any song at a normal tempo. The changes tool keeps your scores, so you can watch them climb.

::tool[Am to Dm changes]{name="changes" a="Am" b="Dm"}

::tool[D to Dm changes]{name="changes" a="D" b="Dm"}

## Strum it

A simpler pattern this time: D – DU D – DU.

::strum{pattern="D - D U D - D U" bpm=70 chord="Dm"}

::exercise{id="am-dm-strums"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Strumming the A string or low E. Dm is a four-string chord.
- The middle finger muting the open D string. Keep it arched over.
- The index finger on fret 1 of the e string flattening and catching the B string.

:::

**Mastery check:** 40 changes in one minute between Am and Dm.
