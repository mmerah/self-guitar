Playing for people is its own skill, and it can be practised. Goal: a three-piece set, recorded in one take, played through whatever happens.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: choose the set and its order (once; then review it weekly).
- 5 minutes: one piece as a performance: record it, no stopping, no second tries.
- 3 minutes: listen back and write down one thing to fix tomorrow.

## Build the set

- **Pick pieces you own.** Each one should run clean at full tempo three times in a row in practice. Performance usually costs you about a fifth of your best.
- **Order them.** Open with your surest piece, not your hardest. Put the most demanding one second, and close with the one people will remember.
- **Vary them.** Change key, tempo or feel from piece to piece: a Travis tune, an arrangement, a groove.
- **Plan the gaps.** Retuning, a capo, a sentence to introduce the next piece.

## Record yourself

Put the phone on a stand about a metre away, pointing where the neck meets the body. Record the whole set in one take. Listen the next day, with fresh ears, and check one thing per listen: timing, the balance of melody and bass, buzzes, rushing at the hard bits. Fix one thing at a time.

## Play through mistakes

The rule on stage: the beat never stops. Practise it on purpose. Play with the metronome and, now and then, skip a chord or fluff a note deliberately, then rejoin cleanly on the next beat 1.

Mark a few rejoin points in each piece, usually the start of each phrase. If you get lost, jump to the next one.

::tool[Metronome for run-throughs]{name="metronome" bpm=80}

## Nerves

Nerves are adrenaline, and they are normal. Before you play, breathe out longer than you breathe in, keep your hands warm, and hear the first bar in your head at the right tempo. Then perform a lot, in small ways: for a friend, on a video call, at an open mic.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Always practising from the top. Start from each rejoin point too, so every part is strong.
- Stopping at mistakes in practice. You will do on stage what you rehearse at home.
- Choosing pieces at the edge of your ability. The set is for showing, not stretching.

:::
