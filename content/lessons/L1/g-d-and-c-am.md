Some changes have a shortcut: a finger that stays put. Finding these "anchor" fingers is the quickest way to faster changes.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute of G–D, then 1 minute of C–Am, every day this week.
- If the anchor finger lifts by habit, play the change at half speed without strumming, eyes on that finger.
- 2 minutes: a slow loop of G G D D C C Am Am, four strums per chord, with the metronome at 60.
- Review: 1 minute of C–G changes.

## C ↔ Am: one finger moves

The index and middle fingers are in the same place in both chords. Only the ring finger travels, between fret 3 of the A string (C) and fret 2 of the G string (Am). Keep the index and middle pressed the whole time.

::chords{ids="C,Am"}

::tool[C to Am changes]{name="changes" a="C" b="Am"}

## G ↔ D: use the four-finger G

Play G with **middle** on the low E, **index** on the A string, **ring** on fret 3 of the B string and **pinky** on fret 3 of the high e. The ring finger now sits exactly where D needs it.

::chord{id="G" voicing="3-2-0-0-3-3"}

::chord{id="D"}

Change to D by keeping the ring finger down: the index lands on fret 2 of the G string, the middle on fret 2 of the high e, and the pinky lifts off. Going back, the ring finger stays and the rest unfold.

::tool[G to D changes]{name="changes" a="G" b="D"}

::strum{pattern="D - D - D - D -" bpm=60 chord="G"}

::tool{name="metronome" bpm=60}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Lifting the anchor finger anyway: then you lose the shortcut.
- With the four-finger G, the middle finger brushing the A string. Arch it.
- Rushing the change and landing late. Slow and on time beats fast and late.

:::

**Mastery check:** 30 changes in one minute between G and D.
