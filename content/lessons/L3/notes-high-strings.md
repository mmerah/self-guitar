Note names let you read melodies, find bass notes and understand chords. Start with the eight notes on the three thinnest strings, all within the first three frets.

## How to practise this

- Say the names aloud: it's the speaking that builds the memory.
- The exercise from 52 BPM up to 80. Play it with a pick or with i and m alternating.
- Quiz yourself: pick a random note name and find it.
- Review: 1 minute of the first-notes exercise on the B and E strings from Level 0.

## The notes

**First position** means the hand sits at the nut with one finger per fret: index for fret 1, middle for fret 2, ring for fret 3.

- **String 3 (G):** open = **G**, fret 2 = **A**
- **String 2 (B):** open = **B**, fret 1 = **C**, fret 3 = **D**
- **String 1 (E):** open = **E**, fret 1 = **F**, fret 3 = **G**

Together they make a run from G up to G: G A B C D E F G.

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 0.3 2.3 0.2 1.2 | 3.2 0.1 1.1 3.1
```

Notice there's no fret 2 note on strings 2 and 1. Between B and C, and between E and F, the notes are only one fret apart; every other pair of neighbouring notes is two frets apart.

## Play a melody

Beethoven's **Ode to Joy** uses only these notes (E F G, C D and G). Open it in the library and read it slowly, naming the notes.

- [Ode to Joy](/play/ode-to-joy)

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Using the middle finger for fret 3 and the hand creeping out of position.
- Playing fret 2 on the B string for C. C is fret 1.
- Guessing the names instead of saying them. Slow down until each name comes instantly.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the exercise at 80 BPM.
