Four barre shapes on the two root strings (6 and 5) give you the 7, m7 and maj7 chords in any key. Goal: vi–ii–V–I in B♭, all barres, at 72 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the four shapes below at fret 3, string by string.
- 6 minutes: the exercise from 46 BPM up to 72.
- Review: 1 minute of D to Bm.

## m7 and maj7 shapes

The **m7** shapes are the minor barres with the 7th added; you met the 7 shapes in the last unit. The **maj7** shapes sound soft and dreamy: the E-shape one skips string 5, so it needs no barre.

::chords{ids="Gm7,Cm7,Gmaj7@3-x-4-4-3-x,Cmaj7@x-3-5-4-5-x"}

::tip[On the E-shape maj7, the index tip mutes string 5 and the middle finger's underside mutes string 1.]

## vi–ii–V–I

A **turnaround** is a short chord run that leads back to the I, ready to start again. The exercise is one in B♭: Gm, Cm7, F7, B♭. The hand stays around frets 1–5 and swaps root strings on every change.

::exercise{id="gm-cm7-f7-bb"}

::ear{kind="chords" set="7,maj7,m7" n=10}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Mixing the root strings up: Gm and F7 have their root on string 6, Cm7 and B♭ on string 5.
- The muted string on maj7 shapes ringing. Let a free finger rest on it.
- Squeezing through four barres in a row. Relax the hand on every beat 4.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of Gm, Cm7, F7, B♭ at 72 BPM.
