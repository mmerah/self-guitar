Slide the box-1 shape down three frets and the same pattern sounds bright and sweet: A major pentatonic. Goal: the major box at fret 2 in 8ths at 84 BPM, and a choice between major and minor over a blues.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the major box, slowly, landing on every A.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 64 BPM.
- 2 minutes: over the 12-bar in A, alternate one chorus of minor (fret 5) and one of major (fret 2).
- Review: one pass of box 1 at 80.

## Same shape, new root

A major pentatonic is A B C♯ E F♯. Those are the same notes as F♯ minor pentatonic, so the box-1 shape at fret 2 plays it. The difference is where you start and stop: on **A**, under the pinky on string 6 and string 1, and under the index on string 3.

::fretboard[A major pentatonic at fret 2]{scale="major-pentatonic" root="A" box="5" labels="intervals"}

Compare the intervals with box 1 of the minor: the major has a 2, 3 and 6 where the minor has a ♭3, 4 and ♭7.

::exercise{id="a-major-pentatonic"}

## Major or minor over a blues?

Over a blues in A, both work, with different moods:

- **Minor (fret 5):** the classic blues sound, gritty. The C clashes with the C♯ of A7, and that rub is the blues.
- **Major (fret 2):** sweet, country and early-rock. Every note fits the A chord.
- **Both:** many players use major over the I chord and switch to minor over the IV and V.

Try the major sound over a happy tune in A:

::song[Cripple Creek in A]{id="cripple-creek" tempo=70}

::fretboard[A minor pentatonic, box 1, for comparison]{scale="minor-pentatonic" root="A" box="1" labels="intervals"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Starting and ending on F♯: then it sounds minor. Lean on A, C♯ and E.
- Playing the major box over a minor song. Over Am, stay with the minor.
- Rushing the stretch from fret 2 to fret 5 on strings 6, 2 and 1: that's the pinky's job.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the A major pentatonic run at 84 BPM.
