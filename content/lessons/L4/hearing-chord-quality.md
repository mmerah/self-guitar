Hearing what kind of chord is playing, and which step of the key it's on, is how players learn songs by ear. Goal: tell major, minor and 7 chords apart, 8 times out of 10.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: play A, Am and A7 yourself, eyes closed, and describe each sound in one word.
- 4 minutes: the chord-quality quiz below, until 20 answers in a row are 80% right.
- 2 minutes: the I–IV–V quiz.
- Review: 1 minute of the sus exercise.

## Three colours

- **Major:** bright, settled.
- **Minor:** darker, sad or serious.
- **7:** major with a twist on top: bluesy, and it wants to move on.

::chords{ids="A,Am,A7"}

::ear{kind="chords" set="major,minor,7" n=20}

## I, IV and V

In a three-chord song, the I is home. The IV lifts away from it, and the V leans back towards it. Hum the root of I before you answer.

::ear{kind="progressions" set="I,IV,V" key="random" n=10}

::tip[Sing the middle note of each chord, the 3rd. In a minor chord it sits a half step lower than in major.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Guessing after the first strum. Let the chord ring and listen to the whole sound.
- Calling every 7 "major": listen for the extra tension on top.
- Practising for 20 minutes in one go. Five minutes a day trains the ear faster.

:::

**Mastery check:** your last 20 answers on major, minor and 7 are at least 80% right.
