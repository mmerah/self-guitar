Sight-reading is playing something new, correctly, the first time. It grows from a little reading every day. Goal: a daily 10-minute habit, reading standard notation from easier library pieces.

## How to practise this

- 30 seconds: the scan below, before every piece.
- 3 minutes: the rhythm exercise (learn it once, then use new rhythms).
- 6 minutes: two pieces you have never played, from well below your level, once each, without stopping.

## The 30-second scan

1. Key and time signature.
2. Highest and lowest notes: which position will you play in?
3. The hardest-looking rhythms: tap them once.
4. Repeats and endings.
5. A tempo you can keep from start to finish. Slow is fine.

## While you play

Keep your eyes one beat ahead of your hands, then one bar. Never stop and never go back: a wrong note is forgiven, a stopped beat is not. Count out loud.

## Reading rhythm

Rhythm is where most reading breaks down, so train it alone. This exercise uses only the open D string.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Repeating a piece until it's memorised. That's practice, not reading; take a new piece.
- Stopping to fix a wrong note. Keep going and land on the next beat.
- Reading too fast. Pick the tempo of the hardest bar.

:::
