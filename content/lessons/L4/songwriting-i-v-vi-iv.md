Four chords, one loop, and your own words on top. Goal: choose the key and capo that suit your voice, then write and sing a four-line verse over I–V–vi–IV.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: the loop in G and C on the ladder, from 58 BPM.
- 3 minutes: find your range and your key (below).
- 7 minutes: write the verse, one line per pass of the loop.
- Review: transpose G D Em C to A with a capo, then without one.

## The loop

I–V–vi–IV is home, lift, the sad turn, then the way back. Played as a loop it never quite ends, which is why it carries so many songs:

- **G:** G, D, Em, C
- **C:** C, G, Am, F
- **D:** D, A, Bm, G

## Find your range

Sing a low "ah" and slide down until it gets breathy or croaky: one step above that is your lowest comfortable note. Do the same going up until it strains. Find both notes on the guitar by sliding up string 5 or string 3 until a note matches.

Many simple verse melodies move between the key note and the 5th above it. A chorus often climbs to the octave.

## Choose the key and capo

Strum G and sing the key note along with string 3, open. Then sing up five notes: do, re, mi, fa, so.

- **Too low** (the bottom feels weak): keep the G shapes and capo up: capo 2 is A, capo 4 is B.
- **Too high** (the top strains): switch to D shapes for D, or C shapes for C. Capo 2 on C shapes gives D, capo 2 on D shapes gives E.

Pick the key where the low notes are clear and the top still feels easy. Write the capo fret next to your lyrics.

::tip[Keys for the same voice differ a lot between people. There's no "right" key, only yours.]

## Write a verse

1. Write four lines, one for each pass of the loop.
2. Speak each line over the strum first. Put the stressed syllables on beats 1 and 3.
3. Sing it with notes from the chord under it. Start on the key note or the 3rd.
4. Rhyme lines 2 and 4, and end the last line on the key note, over the I chord.

Keep the words plain and specific: one picture per line beats four ideas.

## Hear the loop

Loch Lomond turns the same four chords into I–vi–IV–V. Red River Valley uses only I, IV and V. Both are traditional: listen to how each line ends, on I to rest or on V to ask for more.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Choosing the key for the guitar, not the voice. Sing first, then pick the shapes.
- Writing the words before you have a rhythm. Speak them over the strum; cut syllables that won't fit.
- Changing chords early to "help" the melody. Keep the loop steady; the melody does the work.

:::
