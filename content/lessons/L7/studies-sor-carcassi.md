Two studies that every classical guitarist meets: a Sor piece where a melody sings over its own bass, and a Carcassi Allegro in 16th notes. Pick **one**. Goal: the Sor at its full tempo, or the Carcassi at 70 %, start to end.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: listen once with the staff on, marking where each section starts.
- 6 minutes: one section at the current ramp tempo, three clean passes in a row.
- 2 minutes: play the section before and this one together.
- Review: the Carulli waltz, one part at 100 %.

## Sor: Study, Op. 35 No. 2

C major, 3/8, two parts that each repeat. The top voice is the melody: rest strokes with i and m where you can. The bass, with the thumb, is quieter and held for its full length.

::song[Sor, Study Op. 35 No. 2]{id="sor-op35-02" tempo=60}

Plan: Part A at 60 % until clean, then Part B at 60 %, then both at 75 %, 90 %, 100 %. Play the melody alone first, then the bass alone, then both.

## Carcassi: Study, Op. 60 No. 7

A minor, Allegro, in 16th notes from the first bar: a quarter-note bass under repeated 16ths on the treble strings. Form: bars 1–8 once, then bars 9–28 twice. Free strokes throughout; keep the hand still and the fingers close to the strings.

::song[Carcassi, Study Op. 60 No. 7]{id="carcassi-op60-07" tempo=50}

Plan: bars 1–8, then bars 9–28 in four-bar groups, all at 50 %. Then the whole study at 55 %, 60 %, 65 % and 70 %, one step every two or three clean days.

::song[Warm-up study: Carcassi, Op. 60 No. 1]{id="carcassi-op60-01" tempo=70}

::song[Going further: Bach, Prelude BWV 999]{id="bach-prelude-bwv999" tempo=60}

::warning[If one bar always breaks, take it out: loop that bar and the note after it, slowly, until it's the easiest bar in the piece.]

::tip[Record yourself at 100 %. If the recording sounds rushed, the tempo is not mastered yet.]
