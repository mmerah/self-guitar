A harmonic is a bell-like note made by touching a string at exactly the right point instead of pressing it. Goal: clear natural harmonics at frets 12, 7 and 5, and a first artificial harmonic.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: fret-12 harmonics on all six strings, until each one rings clearly.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 48 BPM up to 72.
- 2 minutes: artificial harmonics on the open C chord, below.
- Review: one free-stroke p-i-m-a bar.

## Natural harmonics

Touch the string lightly with a fretting finger directly above the fret wire, not between frets. Pluck, then lift the finger so the note rings on.

- **Fret 12** halves the string: the note one octave above the open string.
- **Fret 7** divides it in three: an octave and a fifth above.
- **Fret 5** divides it in four: two octaves above.

::tip[Plucking near the bridge makes harmonics louder and clearer.]

::exercise{id="natural-harmonics-12-7-5"}

## Artificial harmonics

Any fretted note has a harmonic 12 frets higher. Fret the note as usual; touch the string 12 frets above it with the picking hand's index finger (i) and pluck behind it with the thumb or a.

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 3.5{ah 12} 2.4{ah 12} 0.3{ah 12} 1.2{ah 12} | :2 0.1{ah 12} r
```

Above is the C chord, one artificial harmonic per string: touch at fret 15, 14, 12 and 13, then 12 on the open string 1.

::warning[Don't press at the harmonic point. Pressing at fret 12 gives a fretted note; touching gives the harmonic.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the harmonics exercise at 72 BPM.
