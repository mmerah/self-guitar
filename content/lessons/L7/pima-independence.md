Each finger of the picking hand should move on its own while the others stay still, and the thumb should keep its own time. Goal: the p-i-m-a-m-i arpeggio in free strokes at 90 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: on open strings, play i, m, a one at a time; watch that the fingers not playing don't twitch.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 58 BPM up to 90.
- 1 minute: the same pattern starting a-m-i (reverse), on Am only.
- Review: the rest-stroke scale, once at 80.

## What independence looks like

When **a** plays, **m** and **i** stay where they were, close to their strings. The thumb plays slightly ahead of the fingers, towards the neck, so it never collides with **i**.

The ring finger is tied to the middle by a tendon; it will want to move with it. Slow repetitions with a pause before each **a** teach it to go alone.

## Block (full) planting

As the thumb plays, plant i, m and a together on strings 3, 2 and 1. Each finger then leaves its string in turn. Going back down (m, i), each finger replants just before it plays.

::warning[Free strokes only: a rest stroke here would mute the string below and cut the arpeggio short.]

::tip[Accent the a finger lightly: the top note of the arpeggio is the melody in most studies.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the arpeggio exercise at 90 BPM.
