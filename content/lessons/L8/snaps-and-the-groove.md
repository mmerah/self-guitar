Two more sounds, the snap and the chunk, and then everything at once: the level's groove on four chords at 90 BPM, steady from the first bar to the last.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: snaps alone on string 4, then string 3, until each one cracks at the same volume.
- 3 minutes: chunks and snaps on Am and E7, from 58 BPM.
- 5 minutes: the groove on Em, C, G and D, from 58 BPM.
- Once you pass 90: two minutes nonstop with the metronome. Record it and check that the slaps sit on the click.

## The snap

Hook i under a string, pull it straight up, away from the top, by about half a centimetre, and let go. It slaps back onto the frets with a crack and its own pitch. Classical players call it a Bartók pizzicato; bass players call it a pop. In the tab it's the note with the P mark.

```atex
\tempo 60
:4 0.4 0.4{p} 0.3 0.3{p}
```

## The chunk

A chunk is a muted strum. Ease off the fretting hand so the fingers still touch the strings but don't press them, and flick the nails of i, m and a down across strings 4–1. The tab shows dead notes with a strum arrow.

::exercise{id="snaps-and-chunks-am-e7"}

## The groove

Each bar has eight slots. The thumb plays the four beats: bass, slap, bass, slap. The fingers own the "ands", and the last one is a snap that pushes into the next bar.

```atex
\tempo 70
:8 (0.6 0.2){ch "Em"} 0.3 (x.6 x.5 x.4){s} 0.2 0.6 0.3 (x.6 x.5 x.4){s} 0.3{p}
```

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The snap pulling the groove late. Hook the string a moment early so the release lands on the "and".
- Chunks that ring. If you hear pitch, the fretting hand is still pressing.
- Speeding up when it feels good. Grooves die by rushing; record yourself and check.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the Em–C–G–D groove at 90 BPM.
