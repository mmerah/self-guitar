Your style is the sum of your choices: tunes, sounds, techniques, feel. Goal: learn from three players you love, then write a short piece of your own.

## How to practise this

Over a few weeks, ten minutes a session:

- 3 minutes: one idea from an influence, slowly, by ear.
- 4 minutes: make it yours: new key, new rhythm or new chord under it.
- 3 minutes: work on your piece, one section at a time.

## Three influences

Choose three players, ideally from different corners: a Travis picker, a Piedmont bluesman, a classical guitarist, a modern percussive player, a Celtic DADGAD player. For each one:

1. Transcribe a short passage by ear, using the Level 9 method.
2. Name three things you hear: their tone, their rhythmic feel, a favourite move.
3. Use one of those things in something you already play.

Copy ideas, not whole pieces. Mixing three influences is how a style begins.

## Compose a piece

1. **A seed:** a two-bar idea. A chord change, a groove, a melody, an open tuning.
2. **Section A**, 8 bars: play the idea, repeat it with a change, answer it.
3. **Section B**, 8 bars: a contrast. A new chord, a higher position, a different feel.
4. **Back to A**, and an ending.
5. **Write it down and record it.**

A seed and its first variation: the same rhythm and top notes, moved from Em to C.

```atex
\tempo 80
:8 (0.6 0.1){ch "Em"} 0.3 0.2 0.3 :4 (0.6 3.1) 0.2 | :8 (3.5 0.1){ch "C"} 0.3 1.2 0.3 :4 (3.5 3.1) 1.2
```

Everything from this level is material: a percussive groove, a Travis bass, a Dorian vamp, a ii–V–I.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Waiting for inspiration. Write for ten minutes anyway; the ideas come while you play.
- Editing bar 1 forever. Finish a rough version of all 16 bars first.
- Hiding your influences. Everyone starts by sounding like someone.

:::
