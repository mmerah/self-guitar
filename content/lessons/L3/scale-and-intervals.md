The major scale is the "do re mi" you know from childhood. In C it uses only the natural notes, which you now know on every string. It's also the ruler we use to measure the distance between notes: intervals.

## How to practise this

- The scale exercise from 52 BPM up to 80, singing the note names.
- Play a random pair from the intervals below with your eyes closed. Name the interval.
- When a melody jumps, ask: a step, a third or a fifth?
- Review: 1 minute of the notes on strings 4–6.

## The scale

C D E F G A B C, from fret 3 of string 5 up to fret 1 of string 2:

```atex
\tempo 70
:4 3.5 0.4 2.4 3.4 | 0.3 2.3 0.2 1.2
```

Sing along: "do re mi fa sol la ti do". The half steps (one fret apart) are E–F and B–C; all the other steps are two frets.

::exercise{id="c-major-scale"}

## Three intervals

An interval is the distance between two notes. Count it in scale steps, starting from 1:

- **Step (2nd):** C to D, next door. Sounds like walking.
- **Third (3rd):** C to E, skip one note. Sweet; it's the heart of a chord.
- **Fifth (5th):** C to G, skip three notes. Open and strong, like a horn call.

Each pair below is played one note after the other, then together:

```atex
\tempo 70
:4 3.5 0.4 :2 (3.5 0.4) | :4 3.5 2.4 :2 (3.5 2.4) | :4 3.5 0.3 :2 (3.5 0.3)
```

::tip[Song memory helps: "Twinkle, Twinkle" jumps up a fifth on the second "twin-" (C to G), and steps down from "lit-tle" to "star" (A to G).]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Playing B as fret 1 of string 3. B is the open string 2.
- Counting intervals from 0. C to D is a 2nd: count both notes.
- Rushing the singing. Match the pitch of each note before moving on.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the C major scale exercise at 80 BPM.
