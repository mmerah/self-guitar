Solos don't stay in one box: a slide moves you to the next box without a gap. Goal: box 1 to box 2 and back with a slide on string 3, in 8ths at 100 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the slide alone, ring finger, fret 7 to 9 and back, in time.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 64 BPM up to 100.
- 1 minute: make up your own route, sliding on string 4 or string 2 instead.
- Review: one pass of boxes 4 and 5.

## Where the boxes meet

Boxes 1 and 2 share notes on every string. On string 3, box 1's top note (D, fret 7) is box 2's bottom note, and box 2 goes on to E at fret 9. Slide the ring finger from 7 to 9 and the hand lands in box 2.

::fretboard[Boxes 1 and 2]{scale="minor-pentatonic" root="A" frets="5-10" labels="notes"}

## How to slide

Pick the first note, keep the pressure on, and glide the finger along the string to the new fret. Don't pick the second note: the slide sounds it. Ease off a little while moving, then press again on arrival.

```atex
\tempo 70
:4 7.3{lf 4 sl} 9.3{lf 4} 9.3{lf 4 sl} 7.3{lf 4}
```

::exercise{id="pentatonic-connect-slides"}

::tip[A slide is a rhythm, not a smear: the arrival note lands exactly on its 8th.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Lifting the finger during the slide, so the second note dies.
- Sliding too early, before the first note has had its full length.
- Ending up with the wrong finger in the new box. Slide with the ring, then play box 2 as usual.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the box 1-2 run at 100 BPM.
