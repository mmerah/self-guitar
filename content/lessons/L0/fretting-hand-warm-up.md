A short drill that wakes up all four fretting fingers, teaches them to work one at a time and builds calluses. Use it as the warm-up for every session from here on.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: the drill on the low E string only, very slowly, looking at the pinky.
- 3 minutes: the exercise below with the tempo ladder.
- From now on: 2 minutes of this drill at the start of each session, before anything else.

## The drill: 1-2-3-4

Put one finger per fret: **index on fret 1, middle on 2, ring on 3, pinky on 4**. Play the four frets in order on the low E string, then on each thinner string up to the high e. Then come back down, playing 4-3-2-1 on each string.

::exercise{id="chromatic-warm-up"}

- Keep each finger down until the next string. On the way back, all four fingers start down and lift one at a time.
- Fingertips just behind the fret wire, thumb relaxed behind the neck.
- The pinky will feel weak. That's normal, and this drill is the cure.

## Sore fingertips and calluses

For the first two or three weeks the fingertips will sting. Hard skin (calluses) forms where the strings press, and then it stops hurting. Short daily sessions build calluses faster and more safely than one long one. If a fingertip gets a deep groove or a blister, stop for the day.

## Is the guitar easy to play?

If pressing a string at fret 1 takes a lot of force, the strings may sit too high above the frets (the **action**). A guitar shop can lower it, usually in a few minutes and for little money. It is the single best upgrade for a beginner's guitar.

:::tip
Ten to twenty minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Stop while it still feels good, and leave the guitar out of its case so it's easy to pick up tomorrow.
:::

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Pressing hard with all four fingers at once. Press only as hard as the note needs.
- Fingers flying far off the strings between notes. Keep them hovering close.
- Rushing. The tempo ladder only counts clean passes.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the warm-up at 60 BPM.
