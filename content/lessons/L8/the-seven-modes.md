Play the C major scale from D to D and it sounds minor; from G to G it sounds bluesy. Those are modes. Goal: know each mode's colour note, and play Dorian and Mixolydian phrases over a drone at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: tap through the modes below and play each one up and down from C, leaning on its colour note.
- 5 minutes: the drone exercise on the tempo ladder, from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: the ear quiz on 3rds, 6ths and 7ths.
- 1 minute: improvise in one mode over the open D string. One mode a day.

## Seven modes on one root

Start the C major scale on each of its notes and you get its seven modes: D to D is Dorian, G to G Mixolydian. To hear the colour, play them all on **one** root. Tap a mode; its colour note is highlighted.

::fretboard{modes="all" root="C"}

Bright to dark, each step lowers one note (the colour note in bold):

- **Lydian:** 1 2 3 **♯4** 5 6 7. Floating.
- **Ionian:** 1 2 3 4 5 6 7. The major scale.
- **Mixolydian:** 1 2 3 4 5 6 **♭7**. Bluesy: fiddle tunes, rock.
- **Dorian:** 1 2 ♭3 4 5 **6** ♭7. Minor with a bright 6th.
- **Aeolian:** 1 2 ♭3 4 5 **♭6** ♭7. The natural minor.
- **Phrygian:** 1 **♭2** ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7. Dark: flamenco.
- **Locrian:** 1 ♭2 ♭3 4 **♭5** ♭6 ♭7. Unstable; rarely a home key.

## Dorian and Mixolydian over a drone

The drone keeps D sounding under every note, so you hear each one against home. Only F changes between the two halves: F for Dorian, F♯ for Mixolydian.

::warning[A mode is not a fingering. The C major pattern over a C chord is still C major, whatever note you start on: the bass or drone decides the mode.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the Dorian and Mixolydian drone exercise at 80 BPM.
