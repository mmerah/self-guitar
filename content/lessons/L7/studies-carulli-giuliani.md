A study (étude) is a real piece built around one technique. Learn two short ones the way classical players do: in sections, slowly, then up to tempo. Each session is 10 minutes; a study takes one or two weeks.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: listen to the whole piece once, following the staff.
- 6 minutes: one section only. Play it at the starting tempo below until it is clean three times in a row.
- 2 minutes: join it to the section before.
- Review: one pass of the p-i-m-a arpeggio.

## Carulli: Waltz, Op. 241 No. 4

A G major waltz in four short parts, A to D. The bass takes beat 1 and two chord notes answer it: a dance pattern the thumb and fingers share. Each part is 8 bars and repeats.

::song[Carulli, Waltz Op. 241 No. 4 (start here)]{id="carulli-waltz-op241-04" tempo=60}

The tempo ramp: 60 % until every part is clean, then 75 %, 90 % and 100 %. Move up only after two clean days at the tempo before.

## Giuliani: Allegro, Op. 50 No. 6

A minor, 6/8, with a dotted 16th-and-32nd figure that has to stay crisp. The right hand alternates i and m for the melody and the thumb drops in for the bass.

::song[Giuliani, Allegro Op. 50 No. 6]{id="giuliani-op50-06" tempo=50}

Learn it in four-bar groups: bars 1–4, 5–8, then 9–16 as one phrase. Start at 50 %; the dotted figure must stay long-short, never two even notes.

::song[Next: Carulli, Poco Allegro]{id="carulli-poco-allegro" tempo=60}

::warning[Don't practise only from the start. Begin each session where you stopped last time, so the end of the piece gets as much work as the beginning.]
