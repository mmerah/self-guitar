Add one finger to Em and you have E major. Switching between the two is your first chord change, and the place to learn the two habits that make every change work.

## How to practise this

- Warm-up: 2 minutes of the [finger warm-up](/learn/fretting-hand-warm-up).
- 1 minute of Em–E changes with the timer below.
- 4 minutes of the exercise, from 46 BPM up to 70, using the open-string strum on beat 4.
- Review: 2 minutes of the [Em down-strums](/learn/first-strum) from Level 0.

## E

All six strings ring. **Middle** on fret 2 of the A string (5), **ring** on fret 2 of the D string (4), **index** on fret 1 of the G string (3).

::chords{ids="Em,E"}

Compare it with Em: the middle and ring fingers are in the same place. E is Em plus the index finger on the G string, and that one note turns the chord from minor to major.

::tool[Em to E changes]{name="changes" a="Em" b="E"}

## Habit 1: the strumming hand never stops

When the fretting hand changes chord, the strumming hand keeps swinging down and up in time. If the new chord isn't ready, strum anyway. A slightly messy strum on the beat sounds far better than a gap.

## Habit 2: the open-string strum

On beat 4 before a change, lift the fretting fingers early and strum whatever rings, often just the open strings. That gives the fretting hand a whole beat to reach the next chord, and in a band or a song the open strum is barely noticeable.

::exercise{id="em-e-quarters"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Stopping the strum to change. The beat goes on; the chord catches up.
- The index finger on E leaning on the B string and muting it. Curl it more.
- Lifting all three fingers for Em to E. Only the index moves.

:::

**Mastery check:** 30 changes in one minute between Em and E.
