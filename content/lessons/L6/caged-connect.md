Neighbouring CAGED shapes share notes: the top of one is the bottom of the next. Climb through all five, then pick the chord tones around two of them as arpeggios.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: the five-shape climb from 46 BPM, eyes on the shared notes.
- 5 minutes: the arpeggio exercise from 58 BPM up to 90.
- 1 minute: play one shape, name its root, 3rd and 5th on each string.
- Review: the three-position exercise from the last lesson, once at 80.

## Where they join

::fretboard[All five shapes of C]{caged="all" root="C"}

The A shape's notes at fret 5 are the bottom of the G shape; the G shape's notes at fret 8 are the bottom of the E shape, and so on. When you move up, keep a finger on a shared note and let the rest of the hand follow.

::exercise{id="c-caged-climb"}

## Arpeggios: the chord, one note at a time

An arpeggio plays only the chord tones, root, 3rd and 5th, across all the strings of one shape. The intervals view shows which is which.

::fretboard[C arpeggio, E shape]{chord="major" root="C" frets="7-10" labels="intervals"}

::fretboard[C arpeggio, G shape]{chord="major" root="C" frets="5-8" labels="intervals"}

::exercise{id="c-arpeggio-e-g-shapes"}

::warning[Lift each finger as the next note sounds: arpeggio notes should follow one another, not blur into a strummed chord.]

::tip[Solos that land on these dots sound right over the chord; that is the real use of CAGED.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the two-shape arpeggio at 90 BPM.
