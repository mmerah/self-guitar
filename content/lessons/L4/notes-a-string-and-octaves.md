The A-shape barres coming up are named by their note on string 5. Learn the naturals there, then use octave shapes to find any note a second time, one octave up.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the A-string notes up and down, saying each name.
- 3 minutes: the octave exercise from 46 BPM, naming every pair.
- 3 minutes: quiz rounds on strings 6 and 5 until one takes under 40 s.
- Review: one string-6 quiz round.

## String 5

::fretboard[Naturals on the A string]{notes="naturals" strings="5" frets="0-12" labels="notes"}

Landmarks: A open and at 12, D at 5, E at 7. String 5 at any fret is the note on string 6 five frets higher: fret 3 on the A string (C) matches fret 8 on the low E.

::exercise{id="notes-a-string"}

## Octave shapes

From a note on string 6 or 5, the same note one octave up sits **two strings higher and two frets up**. Index on the low note, ring finger on the high one; the index tip mutes the string in between.

::fretboard[Every C: see the octave pairs]{note="C" frets="0-12" labels="notes"}

Crossing onto string 2 adds a fret, because the B string is tuned a third, not a fourth, above G. From string 4 to 2 or string 3 to 1, the octave is two strings higher and **three** frets up.

::exercise{id="octave-shapes-6-5"}

::warning[Let only the two octave notes sound: a ringing middle string turns the octave into a chord.]

## Quiz

Each round asks seven notes; tap them on string 6 or string 5.

::fretboard{quiz="strings-6-5"}

**Mastery check:** a quiz round on strings 6 and 5 in 40 seconds or less, at most one miss.
