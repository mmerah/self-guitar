Down, up, down, up: one pick stroke per note, never two in the same direction. Goal: box 1 of A minor pentatonic in groups of three, 8th notes at 100 BPM, with strict alternate picking.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: open string 1, slow 8ths, down-up from the wrist. Watch the pick, not the fretboard.
- 3 minutes: the 1-2-3-4 drill on the ladder, from 72 BPM.
- 5 minutes: box 1 in threes, from 64 BPM up to 100.
- Review: one pass of the plain box-1 run, up and down.

## Why down-up

The pick has to come back up anyway. Alternate picking makes that return stroke play the next note, so the hand moves half as far for the same speed.

The rule is simple: downstrokes on the beat, upstrokes on the "&". Keep it even when a passage feels easier another way; the rhythm stays locked to your hand.

## The motion

The stroke comes from the **wrist**, a small turn like shaking water off your hand. The forearm stays still and the fingers only hold the pick.

- Show 1–2 mm of pick tip below your thumb.
- Make the stroke as small as the string allows: just past it, then back.
- Stay loose. Tension is what stops you getting faster.

## The 1-2-3-4 drill

One finger per fret at fret 5, so the fretting hand is easy and you can listen to the pick. Every note has its stroke marked over the tab: ⊓ is a downstroke, V an upstroke.

## Crossing strings: inside and outside

When the next note is on another string, the pick has to get past a string first. There are two cases:

- **Inside:** the pick plays both strings from the gap between them: up on the thicker string, then down on the thinner one (or the reverse).
- **Outside:** it plays them from the outer sides: down on the thicker string, then up on the thinner one (or the reverse).

In the 1-2-3-4 drill every change going up is inside, and every change coming down is outside. Notice which one feels clumsy; that is the one to slow down.

```atex
\tempo 70
\ts (4 4)
:8 5.3{lf 2 sd} 7.4{lf 4 su} 5.3{lf 2 sd} 7.4{lf 4 su} 8.2{lf 5 sd} 5.1{lf 2 su} 8.2{lf 5 sd} 5.1{lf 2 su}
```

Inside first: down on string 3, up on string 4. Then outside: down on string 2, up on string 1.

## Box 1 in threes

Groups of three notes climb the box: notes 1-2-3, then 2-3-4, then 3-4-5, and so on. Each group starts on a different stroke, so you can't fall back on a habit. Every string change is inside picking, and the stroke stays strict down-up from the first note to the last.

::tip[Tap your foot. If a downstroke ever lands off the beat, you have picked two in a row.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Picking from the elbow. The whole arm swings and the speed stalls around 90.
- Two downstrokes in a row after a string change. Say "down, up" out loud for a few passes.
- Gripping harder as the tempo rises. Loosen the grip, keep the stroke small.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of box 1 in threes at 100 BPM.
