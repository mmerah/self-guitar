Every major key has a minor twin that uses the same notes. Goal: name the chords of A minor and E minor, and play i–iv–V7 in both at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: play C then Am, G then Em, and hear the same notes turn sad.
- 5 minutes: i–iv–V7 in A minor and E minor, from 52 BPM.
- 2 minutes: the ear quiz: minor, major or 7?
- Review: name the seven chords of G major, then of C major.

## The relative minor

Start a major scale on its 6th note and you get its **relative minor**: the same notes, a new home. C major and A minor share every note; so do G major and E minor, with their one sharp, F♯.

The relative minor is the vi chord you already know. Its root is three frets below the major root: C to A, G to E.

::chords{ids="C,Am,G,Em"}

## The chords of a minor key

Count from the minor root and the same seven chords get new numbers. Lower case is minor, upper case major:

- **A minor:** Am (i), B° (ii°), C (III), Dm (iv), Em (v), F (VI), G (VII)
- **E minor:** Em (i), F♯° (ii°), G (III), Am (iv), Bm (v), C (VI), D (VII)

They are the chords of C major and G major, starting on Am and Em.

## Why the V is often major

In A minor the v chord is Em: E, G, B. It sounds soft and doesn't pull home. So players raise the 7th note of the scale, G, by a fret to **G♯**. That scale is the **harmonic minor**.

G♯ is one fret below A, and it leans hard towards it. With G♯ the v chord becomes **E major** (E G♯ B), and with a D on top **E7**. In E minor the same trick turns D into D♯: Bm becomes **B** and **B7**.

::chords{ids="Em,E,E7,Bm,B7"}

## i–iv–V7

Home, away, then the strong pull back: Am–Dm–E7–Am in A minor, Em–Am–B7–Em in E minor. The V7 makes the return to i sound inevitable.

::exercise{id="minor-i-iv-v7-am-em"}

## Hear the difference

A minor chord, a major chord or a 7th? The 7th chord is the one that wants to move.

::ear{kind="chords" set="minor,major,7" n=10}

## Play it in a song

Wayfaring Stranger is i, iv and V7 in A minor, nothing else. The Erie Canal sits in E minor, with B7 pulling back to Em.

::song[Wayfaring Stranger: Am, Dm and E7]{id="wayfaring-stranger" tempo=90}

::song[The Erie Canal: E minor]{id="erie-canal" tempo=80}

::tip[Find the key of a minor song from its last chord: it almost always ends on i.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Playing Em where the song says E or E7. The G♯ is the whole point: listen for it.
- Thinking a minor key needs new chords. The i, iv and VI are open chords you know.
- Calling a song in Am "C major" because it uses C and G. Check where it comes to rest.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of i–iv–V7 in A minor and E minor at 80 BPM.
