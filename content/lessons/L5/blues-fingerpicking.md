In country blues the thumb thumps the root on every beat while the fingers play the tune on top. Goal: a fingerpicked 12-bar blues in E at 72 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the thumb alone on open string 6, four even beats, for one minute without stopping.
- 3 minutes: the two-bar thumb drill from 52 BPM.
- 4 minutes: the 12-bar from 52 BPM up to 72.
- Review: one bar of Travis picking on C.

## The monotonic thumb

Unlike Travis picking, the thumb doesn't alternate: it plays **one bass string** on every beat. That steady thump is the drum of the song. Mute it lightly with the side of the picking hand (palm) if it booms.

The fingers (i and m) pick the high strings, sometimes together with the thumb (a pinch), sometimes between beats.

::exercise{id="monotonic-thumb-e"}

## The 12-bar

The thumb follows the chord's root: open E (string 6), open A (string 5), and B (string 5, fret 2) in the B7 bars. Hold A7 and B7 as full shapes, so the fingers find their notes inside them.

::chords{ids="E,A7,B7"}

::exercise{id="fingerpicked-blues-e"}

::tip[Practise the thumb while you talk or count aloud. When it keeps going without you, the fingers are free.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The thumb stops when the fingers get busy. The thumb never stops.
- The thumb speeding up in the easy bars. Play with the click.
- Picking the high notes with the thumb as well. Thumb: string 6 or 5 only.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the fingerpicked blues at 72 BPM.
