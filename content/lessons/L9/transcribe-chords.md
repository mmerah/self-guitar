Chords by ear: find the key, then the bass, then the quality, then the number. Goal: name I, ii, iii, IV, V and vi in a progression, right at least 80% of the time.

## How to practise this

- 3 minutes: the progression quiz.
- 2 minutes: the chord-quality quiz.
- 5 minutes: the chords of one verse from a recording you like.

## The method

1. **Key:** find the home chord, the one the song ends on and keeps returning to. Find its root.
2. **Bass:** hum the lowest note of each new chord and find it on strings 6 and 5.
3. **Quality:** major, minor or 7? You trained this in Level 4.
4. **Number:** name each chord's degree in the key: I, IV, V, vi…
5. **Changes:** count bars. Most songs change chord on beat 1, every bar or every two.
6. **Check:** play along with the recording.

## The shortlist

In a major key, six chords cover most folk and pop songs: I, ii, iii, IV, V and vi. In G they are:

Common patterns are worth knowing by sound: I–IV–V–I, I–V–vi–IV, I–vi–IV–V and ii–V–I.

Practise on the library: play a chord sheet without looking, write down the chords of the first line, then check.

::song[A first test: Down in the Valley]{id="down-in-the-valley" tempo=100}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Mixing up I and vi. They share two notes; the bass note tells them apart.
- Missing a quick chord. If the bass moves for only two beats, there's probably a chord there.

:::

**Mastery check:** 80% right over your last 30 progression answers, with all six chords heard.
