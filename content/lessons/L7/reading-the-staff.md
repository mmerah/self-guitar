Classical studies are written in standard notation, and the tab under it only tells you where, not what. Learn to read the first-position notes from the staff and play a short melody without looking at the tab.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: name the open strings on the staff below, low to high, aloud.
- 5 minutes: the melody exercise from 52 BPM, standard notation on, tab covered with a finger.
- 2 minutes: play any tab in the app with notation on and read the note names before playing.
- Review: one round of the string-6 note quiz.

## The staff and the clef

Five lines, four spaces. The treble clef curls round the second line from the bottom, which makes it G. Lines from the bottom are E G B D F; spaces spell F A C E. Notes below the staff hang on short **ledger lines**.

The guitar's clef has a small 8 under it: the guitar sounds an octave lower than written. Every guitar part works this way, so you can ignore it while reading.

## The open strings

- String 6 (E) hangs below three ledger lines.
- String 5 (A) sits on the second ledger line.
- String 4 (D) sits just under the bottom line.
- String 3 (G) is the second line; string 2 (B) is the middle line.
- String 1 (E) is the top space.

Each step up the staff, line to space, is one letter up. Between E–F and B–C that is one fret; between the others it's two.

## The fretted notes you need

Middle finger on fret 2, ring finger on fret 3:

- String 5: fret 2 is **B**, just under the first ledger line; fret 3 is **C**, on it.
- String 4: fret 2 is **E**, the bottom line; fret 3 is **F**, the first space.
- String 3: fret 2 is **A**, the second space.

## Tab and staff together

In any player, open the ⋯ menu and tap **Standard notation**: the staff appears above the tab, note for note. The staff gives the pitch and the rhythm; the tab gives the string and fret.

```atex
\tempo 60
\staff { score tabs }
:4 0.6 0.5 0.4 0.3 | 0.2 0.1 :2 r
```
