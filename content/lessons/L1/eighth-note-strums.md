Until now your hand came back up without touching the strings. Now it strums on the way up too: twice as many strums, and the start of every strumming pattern in Level 2.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: one chord (G) with the strum player at 60, counting "1 & 2 &" aloud.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 52 BPM, climbing to 80 with clean loops.
- Review: 1 minute of C–G changes.

Keep the strumming arm relaxed. If your forearm burns, you're gripping too hard.

## Down on the beat, up on the "and"

Strum **down** on the numbers and **up** on every "&". Your hand moves like a pendulum: down, up, down, up, never stopping.

::strum{pattern="D U D U D U D U" bpm=60 chord="G"}

The up-strum is lighter and only catches the thinnest three or four strings. Don't aim for the thick strings on the way up.

## Holding the pick

Hold the pick a little looser than for single notes and let only the tip cross the strings. A pick that's too deep catches and drags, especially on the up-strums.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Downs louder than ups by a mile. Ups are lighter, but should still be heard.
- The hand stopping at the bottom or top. It's one continuous motion.
- Strumming from the shoulder. Move from the elbow and a loose wrist.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the exercise at 80 BPM.
