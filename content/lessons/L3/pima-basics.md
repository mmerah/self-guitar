Fingerstyle starts with giving the thumb and each finger its own strings. This lesson only needs Level 1 chords, so you can mix it in alongside Level 2.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: p-i-m-a on open strings, no chord, eyes closed. Feel which finger is on which string.
- 4 minutes: the exercise from 46 BPM up to 70. Every note should be the same volume.
- Let the notes ring into each other: don't lift fretting fingers until the chord changes.
- Review: 1 minute of C–Am changes.

## The letters

The picking-hand fingers have Spanish names, shortened to one letter:

- **p** (pulgar): thumb, plays the bass strings **6, 5 and 4**.
- **i** (índice): index, plays string **3**.
- **m** (medio): middle, plays string **2**.
- **a** (anular): ring, plays string **1**.

The little finger usually rests or stays out of the way.

## Hand position

Rest the forearm on the guitar as for strumming, with the hand over the sound hole. Let the wrist arch slightly so the fingers hang over strings 3, 2 and 1 and the thumb sits a little ahead of them, towards the neck, over the bass strings.

Each finger plucks towards the palm, in a small curl from the knuckle. The thumb pushes down and slightly forward, staying straight.

## Your first pattern: p-i-m-a

Hold a C chord and play the thumb on string 5, then i, m and a on strings 3, 2 and 1.

```atex
\tempo 60
:8 3.5{ch "C"} 0.3 1.2 0.1 3.5 0.3 1.2 0.1
```

For each chord, the thumb plays the root: string 5 for C and Am, string 6 for Em and G. The fingers stay on the same three strings.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The thumb wandering onto string 3, or the fingers onto the bass strings.
- Pulling the strings outward so they slap the frets. Pluck across, towards the palm.
- The hand bouncing. Movement should come from the fingers, not the wrist.

:::

**Mastery check:** a clean pass of the p-i-m-a exercise at 70 BPM.
