Two listening skills make strumming much easier: counting eighth notes so you always know where you are in the bar, and hearing the moment a song moves to a new chord.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: count and clap eighths with the metronome at 60.
- 2 minutes: clap the pattern below, counting all eight slots aloud.
- 2 minutes: the "hear the change" example, then a song from the library.
- Review: 2 minutes of the G–Em–C–D exercise, counting "1 & 2 &" while you strum.

## Counting eighths

A beat split in two is two eighth notes: "1 &". A bar of 4/4 is "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". The numbers are on the beat, where your foot taps; the "&"s are exactly halfway between.

```atex
\tempo 70
:4 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6 | :8 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6 0.6
```

Bar 1 is quarter notes (one per beat); bar 2 is eighth notes (two per beat). Tap your foot and clap along, counting aloud.

::tool[Metronome with 8ths]{name="metronome" bpm=60 sub=2}

## Clap a pattern

Clap only where there's a letter, and say every count, including the silent ones: "**1** & **2 &** **3** & **4 &**".

::strum{pattern="D - D U D - D U" bpm=60}

## Hear the change

Chord changes usually happen on beat 1, often every one or two bars. Listen for the moment the bass note moves and the colour of the music shifts.

Play this and say "change!" each time the chord moves. (G for two bars, then C, then D and Em share the last bar: three changes, one of them halfway through a bar.)

```atex
\tempo 80
:4 (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1){ch "G"} (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) | (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1) | (3.5 2.4 0.3 1.2 0.1){ch "C"} (3.5 2.4 0.3 1.2 0.1) (3.5 2.4 0.3 1.2 0.1) (3.5 2.4 0.3 1.2 0.1) | (0.4 2.3 3.2 2.1){ch "D"} (0.4 2.3 3.2 2.1) (0.6 2.5 2.4 0.3 0.2 0.1){ch "Em"} (0.6 2.5 2.4 0.3 0.2 0.1)
```

Then try it on a real song: play [Scarborough Fair](/play/scarborough-fair) from the library and tap the table each time the chord changes.

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Clapping on "&" when you mean the beat. Your foot taps only on the numbers.
- Counting silently and drifting. Say it out loud until it's automatic.

:::
