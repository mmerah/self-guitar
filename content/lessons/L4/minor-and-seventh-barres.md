Every open shape you know can be barred: Em, E7, Am and A7 become minor and 7 chords anywhere on the neck. Goal: I–vi–ii–V in E with two minor barres at 72 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: each shape below, picked string by string.
- 6 minutes: the exercise from 46 BPM up to 72.
- Review: 1 minute of E to F♯.

## From the E shape (root on string 6)

Lift one finger from the major barre and you get the others:

- **Minor:** lift the middle finger. The index barre now frets string 3 too, like Em.
- **7:** lift the pinky. String 4 drops to the barre, like E7.

At fret 2 they are F♯, F♯m and F♯7.

::chords{ids="F#,F#m,F#7"}

## From the A shape (root on string 5)

The A-shape minor is the Am shape barred: index across the fret, **middle** one fret up on string 2, **ring and pinky** two frets up on strings 4 and 3. The 7 is the A7 shape: ring on string 4, pinky on string 2, the barre frets string 3.

::chords{ids="C#,C#m,C#7"}

The minor 7 versions (m7) do both: lift the finger that makes the 7 as well. You'll use them in the lesson on moveable sevenths.

::exercise{id="e-c-sharp-m-f-sharp-m-b"}

::tip[C♯m to F♯m is a big jump: look at fret 2 while you're still on beat 4 of C♯m.]

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- In the E-shape minor, string 3 buzzes: it sits right in the index crease. Shift the barre slightly.
- In the A-shape minor, the middle finger leans on string 1. Arch it.
- Strumming string 6 on C♯m. Start the strum on string 5.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of E, C♯m, F♯m, B at 72 BPM.
