Syncopation puts the strum where the beat isn't: on an "a", ringing over the next beat. A push moves a chord change a 16th early. Goal: the syncopated groove with its push at 80 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 1 minute: say "1 e & a 2 e & a" and clap only the strums of the pattern below.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 52 BPM up to 80.
- 2 minutes: loop bars 1 and 2 only, until the early C lands without a hiccup.
- Review: one pass of the 16th groove at 90.

## The pattern

You know D – DU – UDU in 8ths. Squeeze it into two beats of 16ths and it becomes syncopated: the up-strum on the "a" of 1 rings over beat 2, and beat 2 itself is a miss.

```atex
\tempo 60
:8 (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1){bd} :16 (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1){bd} :8 (0.3 0.2 3.1){bu} :16 (0.3 0.2 3.1){bu} (3.6 2.5 0.4 0.3 0.2 3.1){bd} (0.3 0.2 3.1){bu} :2 r
```

## The push

In bars 1 and 3 the last up-strum, on the "a" of 4, is already the next chord. It rings over beat 1 of the next bar (the tie in the tab, a miss in the strum row), so the change arrives early and the bar line feels pulled forward.

::strum{pattern="D-DU -UDU D-DU -UDU --DU -UDU D-DU -UDU" bpm=60 chord="G"}

::exercise{id="syncopated-sixteenths"}

::warning[Keep the hand moving on the missed beats. If it stops on beat 2, the next up-strum comes late.]

::tip[Accent the pushed strum slightly: it's the one the listener should feel.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the syncopated exercise at 80 BPM.
