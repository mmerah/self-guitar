Piedmont blues, the style of players like Blind Boy Fuller and Rev. Gary Davis, puts an alternating thumb under ragtime chords and blue notes. Goal: the ragtime cycle at 90 BPM, and two licks that never stop the thumb.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the thumb alone through C, A7, D7 and G7.
- 4 minutes: the ragtime cycle on the tempo ladder, from 58 BPM.
- 3 minutes: the licks on the tempo ladder, from 58 BPM.
- 1 minute: alternate two bars of plain thumb on E7 with two bars of licks.

## The ragtime cycle

C – A7 – D7 – G7 is I – VI7 – II7 – V7. Each chord is the dominant 7 of the next one: A7 pulls to D, D7 to G, G7 home to C. The roots go round the circle of fifths.

The thumb alternates between the root and another chord tone on each beat, Travis-style. On C, the ring finger hops from the C on string 5 to the G on string 6 for beat 3: that rolling bass is the Piedmont sound.

## Licks with the thumb still going

Blues licks over an alternating thumb are hard because the fretting hand is busy. A trick: play E7 with open E and open D in the bass. The thumb needs no fretted notes, so the left hand is free.

The blue notes in E are G (the ♭3), B♭ (the ♭5) and D (the ♭7). Bending, hammering or sliding from a blue note up to its neighbour is the classic move.

::fretboard[E blues scale, open position]{scale="blues" root="E" box="1"}

::exercise{id="e7-thumb-licks"}

Use the licks in a 12-bar in E: bars 1–4, 7–8 and 11 are all on E, so the thumb can stay on open strings while you play them.

::song[A blues in E to try them in]{id="poor-boy-long-way-from-home" tempo=100}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- The thumb waiting for the lick. Practise at 58 BPM until the bass never hesitates.
- Hammer-ons landing late. The hammered note falls exactly on the "and".
- A booming bass. Rest the picking hand's edge lightly on the bass strings for a tight thump.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the ragtime cycle at 90 BPM.
