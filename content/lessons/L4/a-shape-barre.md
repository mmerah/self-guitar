The A shape moved up the neck gives you major chords with the root on string 5. Goal: a ringing B at fret 2, and I–IV–V in E at 76 BPM.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the partial barre below, then the full one, at fret 3 (C).
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 50 BPM up to 76.
- Review: 1 minute of E to F♯ from the last lesson.

## The shape

Play A with **middle, ring and pinky** on fret 2 of strings 4, 3 and 2. Move it up two frets and lay the index across fret 2 from string 5 to string 1: that's **B**. The note under the index on string 5 names the chord.

::chord{id="B" voicing="x-2-4-4-4-2"}

Fret 1 is B♭, fret 3 is C, fret 5 is D.

::chords{ids="Bb,C#"}

::chord{id="C" voicing="x-3-5-5-5-3"}

## A partial barre first

The three fingers are crowded on fret 4. Start without the high e:

1. Index on fret 3 of string 5 only; middle, ring, pinky on fret 5 of strings 4, 3, 2. Strum strings 5 to 2: a C chord on four strings.
2. Then flatten the index across all five strings so it also frets string 1.

Many players fret strings 4, 3 and 2 with a **ring-finger mini-barre** instead. It's faster but the high e is often muted. Either way is fine; play what rings.

## String 6

String 6 isn't part of the chord. Touch it lightly with the tip of the index (the one under string 5) so it can't ring, and aim your strum from string 5.

::exercise{id="e-a-b-barre"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Strumming string 6: the chord sounds muddy. Mute it with the index tip.
- The three fingers on fret 4 crowding each other so string 2 dies. Angle the pinky in.
- Changing from A to B by lifting everything. Keep the shape and just move it.

:::

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of E, A, B, E at 76 BPM.
