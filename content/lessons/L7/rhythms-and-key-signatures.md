Note shapes and rests tell you how long, the time signature how the beats group, and the sharps or flats at the start of a line which notes are always raised or lowered. Read them in one 8-bar melody in G.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: clap the melody's rhythm while counting "1 & 2 &" aloud.
- 5 minutes: the exercise from 52 BPM, notation on.
- 2 minutes: find the key signature of three library songs; say which notes it changes.
- Review: the reading melody in C, once.

## Note values and time signatures

You met note values, dots and triplets in Level 3; on the staff they look the same. The time signature's top number says how many beats are in a bar; the bottom says which note is one beat (4 = a quarter).

**Compound meter** groups 8th notes in threes. **6/8** has six 8ths a bar, felt as two big beats: count "**1** 2 3 **4** 5 6". **3/8** has three 8ths, felt as one beat: many Sor studies are in it. A dotted quarter fills one big beat.

## Key signatures

Sharps or flats after the clef apply to every note of that letter, in every octave, for the whole piece. One sharp (on the F line) is G major or E minor: every F becomes F sharp. One flat (on the B line) is F major or D minor.

Sharps always appear in the order F C G D A E B; flats in the order B E A D G C F. Two sharps, F and C, is D major.

::warning[An F with no sign in a piece in G is still F sharp: the key signature counts, even when nothing is printed next to the note.]

**Mastery check:** three clean passes in a row of the G melody at 80 BPM.
