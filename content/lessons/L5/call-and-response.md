Blues is a conversation: a phrase asks, the next one answers, and the silence between them is part of the music. Goal: improvise a full 12-bar chorus built from calls and responses.

## How to practise this

- 2 minutes: the call-and-response exercise from 58 BPM.
- 3 minutes: make up your own calls; answer each one twice, differently.
- 4 minutes: improvise over the blues song below, one chorus at a time.
- Review: bars 1–4 of the 12-bar solo.

## Question and answer

A **call** goes up and stops on an unsettled note, like G or C: it sounds like a question. The **response** comes down and lands on the root, A: the answer. Rest a bar after each.

::exercise{id="call-and-response-a"}

## Three rules for phrasing

1. **Space:** play a bar, rest a bar. Rests let the listener hear what you said.
2. **Repetition:** play a good phrase again, maybe with a small change. Listeners love to recognise it.
3. **Few licks, well played:** three phrases you can play with feeling beat twenty you can't.

## A full chorus

Twelve bars is three calls and three responses: one pair per line of the form. Use the licks you know: the bend from bar 1 of the solo, the legato run, the turnaround line.

Play along with this 12-bar blues in E. Use box 1 at fret 12, the same shape as A at fret 5, or at fret 0 with the open strings. On a first try, solo while the song plays and strum it on the next chorus.

::song[Poor Boy, Long Way from Home: 12-bar in E]{id="poor-boy-long-way-from-home" tempo=90}

::fretboard[E minor pentatonic, box 1 at the nut]{scale="minor-pentatonic" root="E" box="1" labels="intervals"}

:::warning
Common mistakes:

- Playing all the time. If you can't sing your solo back, there's too much in it.
- Running up and down the box like the exercise. Use 3–5 notes per phrase.
- Stopping when you hit a "wrong" note. Bend or slide it to a neighbour and keep going.

:::
